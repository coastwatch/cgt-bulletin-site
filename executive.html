What this means for the market

  • The sector is shifting from platform hype to operational discipline.
  • Cell therapy sentiment is generally stronger than broad gene therapy sentiment.
  • Differentiated assets still command capital; weaker stories are punished quickly.
  • Partners who reduce execution risk are becoming more valuable.

Most important conclusion

The next winners in CGT will not just have compelling science. They will have better evidence, cleaner manufacturing, stronger regulatory packages, and more executable commercial models.

Life Biosciences reports first-in-human ER-100 Phase 1 data in glaucoma

The company reports ER-100 was tolerated through Day 56 in three participants with open-angle glaucoma, with preliminary visual-field improvement signals in two; its safety-focused Phase 1 continues, and the monitoring board recommended dose escalation. The very small, uncontrolled cohort makes this an early signal—not evidence of efficacy—but highlights clinical translation of epigenetic cellular reprogramming.

AAO 2026: four-year MCO-010 follow-up reports sustained vision gains in retinitis pigmentosa

Ophthalmology Times reports that the REMAIN follow-up from the Phase 2b/3 RESTORE study found mean best-corrected visual-acuity gains sustained at week 204 in the two dose groups; the original study randomized 27 patients. This is conference-reported long-term follow-up for an optogenetic cell-targeting therapy, not an FDA-approved treatment; interpret as reported clinical data and confirm details against the meeting presentation.

🌐 Peer-reviewed clinical studyOctober 13, 2026
Phase I study reports rapid, point-of-care trispecific CAR-T manufacturing for B-cell cancers

A first-in-human study reported a trispecific CAR-T targeting CD19/CD20/CD22 with OX40 costimulation, using a six-day collection-to-infusion workflow. Eighteen patients enrolled and 16 were treated; early activity across lymphoma subtypes is promising but the small Phase I cohort is not definitive. The operational signal is notable: rapid fresh-cell manufacture may offer a distinct alternative to longer centralized production timelines.

Lakefront sells preclinical CAR-T programs to Armed Cell Therapeutics

Lakefront (formerly Galapagos) completed the sale of its early-stage cell-therapy pipeline to the newly formed Armed Cell Therapeutics: €8 million upfront, up to $230 million in milestones, plus a low-single-digit royalty. The transferred assets include multi-targeted/armed CAR-T constructs for hematologic and solid tumors. The deal completes Lakefront’s planned cell-therapy wind-down while giving the programs a new independent sponsor; all assets remain preclinical.
