Bottom Line

CGT is still strategically investable, but the market now rewards execution far more than novelty. As of October 24, the cycle's defining feature is that the sector's two fastest-growing narratives — autoimmune CAR-T and off-the-shelf cell therapy — both took material hits: a patient death in BMS's autoimmune zola-cel program, and Roche walking away from the off-the-shelf CAR-T asset in its up-to-$1.5B Poseida deal. Underneath, the science is genuinely strong (ESMO's PRAME and TIL data, first-line lifileucel), but the binding constraint on growth is no longer scientific or manufacturing capacity. It is safety, access, and whether an off-the-shelf modality makes the autologous manufacturing model unnecessary.

Undetectable pgRNA is a marker, not a cure — and the follow-up runs to 2040

Precision BioSciences presented Phase 1 data from ELIMINATE-B showing that its in vivo gene editor PBGENE-HBV achieved complete and durable loss of pgRNA to undetectable in 100% of evaluable patients at the go-forward 0.4 mg/kg dose, with durable ongoing pgRNA loss in all patients who had at least six months of follow-up. The company frames this as indicative of permanent elimination of cccDNA — covalently closed circular DNA, the persistent viral reservoir that current antivirals do not clear — and says the programme is advancing to a global Phase 2 study to evaluate cure rates after a finite course of treatment and cessation of nucleos(t)ide analogues. The supporting details as reported: across all dose levels, 11 evaluable patients achieved pgRNA loss to undetectable and remained undetectable through the data cutoff; a first HBeAg-positive patient dosed on study went from a baseline of 3.7-log copies/mL to undetectable; and liver biopsies showed a reproducible 1-log and 1.5-log reduction in cccDNA at 0.4 mg/kg across multiple patients. Treatment effect was observed in 100% of evaluable patients as measured by HBsAg reduction. Safety was described as well characterised and manageable across 59 administrations in 25 patients, with no dose-limiting toxicities. Data cutoff September 28, 2026; the late-breaking oral presentation is November 8 at AASLD, and the company expects to publish a Phase 1b data set in the first half of 2027. Registry: NCT06680232 is a recruiting Phase 1, open-label, first-in-human dose-escalation and expansion study of PBGENE-HBV, 45 estimated participants, sponsor Precision BioSciences, actual start November 14, 2024, estimated primary completion December 2026, last update posted August 24, 2026, seven sites across France, Hong Kong, Moldova, New Zealand, Romania and the United States. A long-term observational follow-up, NCT07254208, is enrolling by invitation with 45 estimated participants and primary completion January 2040. The boundaries carried in the item: undetectable pgRNA is a viral marker, not a durability or safety demonstration — the follow-up study runs to 2040 precisely because the durability question is open — and the company states it will be presenting, not that it has presented, these results.

A safety milestone in one patient — and a different dystrophin strategy from the approved field

The independent Data Safety Monitoring Board for Precision BioSciences’ PBGENE-DMD cleared the first patient treated in the Phase 1/2 FUNCTION-DMD study for Duchenne muscular dystrophy, with no serious adverse events and no adverse events of special interest through week eight, and endorsed proceeding with dosing additional patients in Part 1. The company describes the eight-week window as the period that matters for monitoring the early safety events known to be associated with AAV-based therapies. The mechanism is the part that distinguishes this from the approved microdystrophin products. Spatial control of a cytokine payload instead of more engineering per patient

A Nature Cancer paper published November 5, 2026 describes a way to make a solid-tumour CAR T-cell deliver a cytokine only where and when it is needed. The problem being addressed is the one that has most limited CAR T-cell therapy in solid tumours: poor infiltration and impaired effector function, while radiotherapy is constrained by radioresistance and metastatic spread. The authors combine the spatial precision of ionizing radiation with the biological specificity of CAR T-cells to achieve localized delivery of tumor necrosis factor (TNF). They identified radiation-responsive genes in tumor-infiltrating T cells and engineered synthetic radioinducible promoters to drive TNF expression; human CAR T-cells carrying the construct (riTNF CAR-T cells) secreted high levels of TNF upon irradiation, independently of antigen engagement — which matters, because antigen-independent cytokine release is the mechanism that makes the spatial control work. Results as reported: in human xenograft and murine tumor models, the combination of riTNF CAR-T cells with local irradiation produced greater tumor regression than either treatment alone, or standard CAR T-cells with irradiation. The response was associated with high intratumoral but not systemic TNF levels, increased tumor necrosis and apoptosis, vascular disruption and greater CAR T-cell infiltration. The authors also report a limitation worth stating: maximal efficacy required irradiation of all tumor sites, despite macrophage-associated abscopal responses following single-lesion irradiation. What this is and is not: this is preclinical work in human xenograft and mouse tumor models, with no human administration; the construct uses a synthetic promoter derived from the mouse Phlda3 promoter, and RNA sequencing data are deposited in GEO under GSE304882 and GSE305175. A registry sweep returns no registered study of irradiation-controlled cytokine release, so nothing is in the clinic. Why it is worth carrying for a manufacturing reader: this is a different design philosophy from the in vivo editing programmes — the payload is not edited into the genome but switched on by an external signal, which keeps the manufactured product constant and moves the control to the treatment protocol. That reframes the release-testing question: if the product is a fixed cell line with a inducible promoter, the lot-release assay is measuring a construct and a basal state rather than a per-patient potency, and the tumour-directed activity is delivered by radiotherapy scheduling rather than by product manufacture.

What the Federal Register notice states about the final guidance: a potency assurance strategy is a multifaceted approach that reduces risks to the potency of a product through (1) manufacturing process design, (2) manufacturing process control, (3) material control, (4) in-process testing, and (5) potency lot release assays — the goal being to ensure that every lot released has the specific ability or capacity to achieve the intended therapeutic effect. 🌐 Trade feature (in partnership)August 10, 2026
When the vector becomes the drug: the manufacturing shift from ex vivo to in vivo, from AGC Biologics

A trade feature published in partnership with AGC Biologics describes how viral vector manufacturing changes when the vector becomes the drug rather than a manufacturing tool — the most directly useful industry-level treatment of in vivo capacity in this cycle. Three points, all attributed to named AGC personnel: in ex vivo therapy the vector is a tool and the final product is the engineered cell, so impurities can be mitigated downstream; in vivo, the vector is injected directly with no further purification opportunity, so purity requirements are far stricter and regulatory flexibility far lower, with endotoxin, mycoplasma, residual DNA and host cell protein held to injectable-biologic standards. Regulatory treatment follows the same split — the EMA typically classifies ex vivo vectors as starting materials, and the FDA treats them as drug substances while allowing a risk-based approach because the final product is the cell. Scale is the commercial story: ex vivo needs lower vector doses, in vivo needs orders of magnitude more, so bioreactors must scale from 200 to 2,000 litres, transfection-complex mixing needs dedicated procedures, and downstream steps often need re-optimization because column and membrane formats do not scale linearly — with equipment layout and process flow to be planned six months to a year ahead. And the producer cell line changes character: HEK293T reliance carries the SV40 large T antigen, an oncoprotein interfering with p53 and RB, which downstream purification mitigates in ex vivo work but which becomes a live safety concern in vivo, where even trace residual LTA DNA or protein may matter. Caveat: this is a sponsored feature and the framing reflects a CDMO's commercial interests.
