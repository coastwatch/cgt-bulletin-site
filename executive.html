Bottom Line

CGT is still strategically investable, but the market now rewards execution far more than novelty. As of October 24, the cycle's defining feature is that the sector's two fastest-growing narratives — autoimmune CAR-T and off-the-shelf cell therapy — both took material hits: a patient death in BMS's autoimmune zola-cel program, and Roche walking away from the off-the-shelf CAR-T asset in its up-to-$1.5B Poseida deal. Underneath, the science is genuinely strong (ESMO's PRAME and TIL data, first-line lifileucel), but the binding constraint on growth is no longer scientific or manufacturing capacity. It is safety, access, and whether an off-the-shelf modality makes the autologous manufacturing model unnecessary.

What this means for the market

  • The sector is shifting from platform hype to operational discipline.
  • Cell therapy sentiment is generally stronger than broad gene therapy sentiment.
  • Differentiated assets still command capital; weaker stories are punished quickly.
  • Partners who reduce execution risk are becoming more valuable.

Most important conclusion

The next winners in CGT will not just have compelling science. They will have better evidence, cleaner manufacturing, stronger regulatory packages, and more executable commercial models. The October 22–24 data sharpened each of those: a patient death in a Phase 1 autoimmune trial makes the regulatory and safety bar higher, Roche's retreat from off-the-shelf CAR-T raises the bar for allogeneic platforms, Immatics' parallel PRAME bispecific and cell therapy raises the bar for autologous, and McKesson's finding that only about two in ten CAR-T-eligible patients actually receive treatment raises the bar for commercial models. Notably, the access gap is not a manufacturing problem — travel burden, site geography, and reimbursement friction are the real bottlenecks, and the sector is still building for the demand it hopes for rather than the demand it has.

Immatics at ESMO: IMA203CD8 PRAME cell therapy cORR 55% in gynecologic cancers; off-the-shelf IMA402 bispecific 32% cORR in melanoma

Immatics reported updated Phase 1 data for its PRAME franchise. IMA203CD8 (one-time infusion TCR-T) achieved 51% ORR and 41% confirmed ORR across 90 evaluable heavily pretreated patients, with cORR of 55% and median DOR of 8.4 months in 20 gynecologic cancer patients at clinically relevant doses; safety manageable, MTD not reached, no Grade 5 events, and a pediatric investigator-initiated trial planned for 1H 2027. McKesson 2026 CGT Report: only ~2 in 10 CAR-T-eligible patients receive treatment; autoimmune readiness is the weak link

InspiroGene by McKesson's third annual report, based on 200 US physicians, 35 payer leaders covering ~295 million lives, and patient/advocate input, finds only about two in ten CAR-T-eligible patients actually receive treatment, with travel and lodging costs and coverage delays the most cited reasons referred patients never get treated. Quell's CHILL trial delivers the first engineered nTreg cell therapy for autoimmune disease at Oxford

The CHILL trial (NCT07473154) dosed its first patient at the Oxford EMCRF — the second site globally to administer an engineered regulatory T cell therapy for autoimmune disease. Two participants recruited at Oxford University Hospitals after the first at Leeds. The product is QEL-005, an autologous engineered Treg therapy transduced with a lentiviral vector and engineered with a CD19 B-cell activated receptor. Chief Investigator Christopher Buckley's framing is the strategic signal: "CAR-T cells kill; they do not regulate." As the killing modality accumulates deaths in autoimmune disease, a regulatory modality that suppresses rather than depletes is a materially different thesis — and a different manufacturing profile from cytotoxic CAR-T.

🌐 Peer-reviewed clinical studyOctober 24, 2026
BMC Cancer: pre-infusion score predicts post-CAR-T blood count crashes; grade 3–4 neutropenia in 72% by day 4

A 96-patient retrospective cohort at Beijing Tongren Hospital in BMC Cancer maps hematologic toxicity after CAR-T using the ICAHT framework. Grade 3–4 neutropenia peaked at day 4 in 71.9% of patients, with 34.7% still severe at day 28. The pre-infusion CAR-HEMATOTOX score was the only significant predictor of severe early neutropenia (aOR 4.04) and the strongest predictor of early and late thrombocytopenia (aOR 8.22 and 4.20). Bridging therapy predicted late neutropenia (aOR 4.25). G-CSF and thrombopoietin receptor agonists were not associated with increased grade ≥3 CRS or neurotoxicity, removing a common objection to early count support. With CRS (74%, but grade ≥3 in only 2.1%) and ICANS (10.4%) now largely manageable in experienced centers, hematologic recovery has become the dominant safety concern — and a largely predictable one. Single-center retrospective design and n=96 limit the claims.

Asian Lymphoma Study Group updates mantle cell lymphoma consensus: CAR T access concentrated in major academic centers

A 16-author panel across 12 countries and territories published an updated mantle cell lymphoma consensus in Cancer Research and Treatment, scaling treatment intensity explicitly to local access. Its observation that CAR T-cell therapy remains concentrated in major academic centers across the region — so eligibility often depends as much on geography as on disease biology — is an independent confirmation of the access thesis in the McKesson report, from a non-US vantage point. APAC CAR T demand will be built around a small number of academic centers per country, favoring regional manufacturing and centralized logistics over broad site-of-care expansion. Consensus statement, not a systematic review or new comparative data.
