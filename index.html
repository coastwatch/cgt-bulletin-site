Morning update — Oct 25 (8:15 AM EDT) — FIRST ALLOGENEIC CAR-T DATA IN PROGRESSIVE MS, ARPA-H FUNDS THE END OF THE COLD CHAIN, first big-pharma in vivo CAR-T autoimmune trial, and two corrections to published claims: all timestamps refreshed. Four new source-verified items, plus two corrections to claims published earlier this morning. TG Therapeutics presented the first clinical evaluation of an allogeneic CAR-T in progressive MS — azer-cel (azercabtagene zapreleucel, licensed from Precision BioSciences), late-breaking oral at MSToronto2026. In 26 patients (six dose levels, bendamustine lymphodepletion, median 4.9-month follow-up): no DLTs, no Grade ≥3 CRS, no Grade ≥2 ICANS, no GvHD, no Grade ≥3 infections; EDSS stable or improved in 89% (16/18) at Day 90 and 88% (7/8) at Day 180, with three patients improving ≥2.0 EDSS points; CSF CD19+ B-cell events down a median 52.3% at Day 10 and peripheral B cells below 10/µL in 96% — a depth of CNS B-cell depletion that approved anti-CD20 antibodies do not achieve, in a population with no options after B-cell therapy failure. Caveats: 26 patients, uncontrolled, clinician-rated EDSS, durability unproven. ARPA-H is funding the end of the cell therapy cold chain — up to $36.5M to UC Davis-led CYBORGEL testing intracellular polymer gels for room-temperature cell storage (goal: three months usable), with CaseBioscience (up to $7.3M) and Draper ($7.3M) also funded under the BoSS program. ARPA-H states the cold chain "adds tens of billions of dollars in cost every year" and that a dose worth hundreds of thousands of dollars "can be ruined from just one power outage." Strategically the most underrated item this cycle: if therapies move at room temperature, infusion-site economics invert, the distribution model changes, and value shifts from the per-patient cell to a campaign-manufactured stabilized product — a direct subtraction from autologous CDMO slot utilization. Research funding, not demonstrated capability. Correction 1 — the "world's first" in vivo CAR-T autoimmune claim is false. At least four earlier such trials exist, nearly all Chinese academic: OL-CD19-GDT (recruiting since Oct 2025), LNP-mRNA in vivo CAR-T (Jan 2026), in vivo CAR-T in refractory Graves' disease (Jan 2026), and a CD8-targeted LNP CD19 CAR mRNA study in five refractory SLE patients including four with lupus nephritis. Correction 2 — "first big pharma" also needs narrowing. AbbVie already has an in vivo CAR-T autoimmune trial running through its $2.1B acquisition of Capstan: NCT06917742, CPTX2309 in healthy volunteers and in moderate-to-severe RA and SLE, recruiting since April 2025. The accurate framing is that BMS-986553 is the first large-cap pharma to run a dedicated in vivo CAR-T autoimmune Phase 1 as a sponsored, named commercial product — a real distinction, but not "world's first." Nepal treated its first patient with locally manufactured CAR-T, with Dr. Luca Gattinoni (LIT- Leibniz) framing it as a model for low- and middle-income countries — a direct challenge to centralized-manufacturing economics. Charles River, Labcorp and the FDA signed a research collaboration to validate standardized preclinical abuse-potential assessment methods — a preclinical and regulatory-science item, labeled as such.
