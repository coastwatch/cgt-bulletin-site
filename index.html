Evening update — Oct 25 — FIRST IN-BODY PRIME EDITING DOSED IN A PERSON, MARENGO'S T-CELL ENGAGER READS OUT EIGHT TUMOR TYPES, FIRST ALLOGENEIC CAR-T DATA IN PROGRESSIVE MS, ARPA-H FUNDS THE END OF THE COLD CHAIN, and nine corrections to published claims: all timestamps refreshed. Eleven new source-verified items, plus eleven corrections to published claims — two of them narrated below, the rest from later in the day. TG Therapeutics presented the first clinical evaluation of an allogeneic CAR-T in progressive MS — azer-cel (azercabtagene zapreleucel, licensed from Precision BioSciences), late-breaking oral at MSToronto2026. In 26 patients (six dose levels, bendamustine lymphodepletion, median 4.9-month follow-up): no DLTs, no Grade ≥3 CRS, no Grade ≥2 ICANS, no GvHD, no Grade ≥3 infections; EDSS stable or improved in 89% (16/18) at Day 90 and 88% (7/8) at Day 180, with three patients improving ≥2.0 EDSS points; CSF CD19+ B-cell events down a median 52.3% at Day 10 and peripheral B cells below 10/µL in 96% — a depth of CNS B-cell depletion that approved anti-CD20 antibodies do not achieve, in a population with no options after B-cell therapy failure. Caveats: 26 patients, uncontrolled, clinician-rated EDSS, durability unproven. ARPA-H is funding the end of the cell therapy cold chain — up to $36.5M to UC Davis-led CYBORGEL testing intracellular polymer gels for room-temperature cell storage (goal: three months usable), with CaseBioscience (up to $7.3M) and Draper ($7.3M) also funded under the BoSS program. ARPA-H states the cold chain "adds tens of billions of dollars in cost every year" and that a dose worth hundreds of thousands of dollars "can be ruined from just one power outage." Strategically the most underrated item this cycle: if therapies move at room temperature, infusion-site economics invert, the distribution model changes, and value shifts from the per-patient cell to a campaign-manufactured stabilized product — a direct subtraction from autologous CDMO slot utilization. Research funding, not demonstrated capability. Correction 1 — the "world's first" in vivo CAR-T autoimmune claim is false. At least four earlier such trials exist, nearly all Chinese academic: OL-CD19-GDT (recruiting since Oct 2025), LNP-mRNA in vivo CAR-T (Jan 2026), in vivo CAR-T in refractory Graves' disease (Jan 2026), and a CD8-targeted LNP CD19 CAR mRNA study in five refractory SLE patients including four with lupus nephritis. Correction 2 — "first big pharma" also needs narrowing. AbbVie already has an in vivo CAR-T autoimmune trial running through its $2.1B acquisition of Capstan: NCT06917742, CPTX2309 in healthy volunteers and in moderate-to-severe RA and SLE, recruiting since April 2025. The accurate framing is that BMS-986553 is the first large-cap pharma to run a dedicated in vivo CAR-T autoimmune Phase 1 as a sponsored, named commercial product — a real distinction, but not "world's first." Two further items landed this cycle. Vyriad opened the first US Phase 1 of an in vivo BCMA CAR-T delivered by a T-cell-targeted lentiviral vector (NCT07802717, 40 patients, recruiting at Mayo Clinic). A registry sweep finds 20 in vivo lentiviral CAR-T studies worldwide — 18 with Chinese sites, one at an Indonesian hospital, and this the only one with a US sponsor and US sites. AbelZeta has a company-described registrational Phase 2 in lupus nephritis for an autologous CAR-T (NCT07729826, FDA RMAT clearance, 50 patients, 104-week follow-up, Complete Renal Response as the primary endpoint), also holding EMA PRIME, across seven registry records for the same bispecific product, though the records do not correspond to seven distinct active trials — one is withdrawn and several are early-phase or exploratory — and including a 119-patient Phase 1b/2 in progressive MS. Two more items landed in the evening refresh. Prime Medicine dosed the first patient with in-body prime editing — PM577a, an LNP-delivered prime editor for Wilson disease targeting the p.H1069Q ATP7B mutation, is the first reported in vivo prime-editing therapy dosed in a person. No cell extraction, no ex vivo editing, no reinfusion; NCT07748403, 42 participants, recruiting. No safety or efficacy data yet. Marengo's invikafusp alfa read out eight tumor types at ESMO 2026 — 62 evaluable TMB-H solid tumor patients, 56% tumor shrinkage, 18% ORR, 60% DCR, no ICANS and no Grade 5 treatment-related adverse events in 104 safety patients — and the FDA granted a second Fast Track expanding it to TMB-H solid tumors regardless of tissue of origin. It is not a cell therapy product, which is exactly why it belongs here: a non-cell-based way of engaging T cells, with no harvest, no manufacturing slot and no autologous product to ship. Nepal treated its first patient with locally manufactured CAR-T, with Dr. Luca Gattinoni (LIT- Leibniz) framing it as a model for low- and middle-income countries — a direct challenge to centralized-manufacturing economics. Charles River, Labcorp and the FDA signed a research collaboration to validate standardized preclinical abuse-potential assessment methods — a preclinical and regulatory-science item, labeled as such. Two more items landed in the final scan, both peer-reviewed, and both correct an earlier read of them as commentary rather than primary research. A Blood paper from MD Anderson with Sanofi co-authors put a mechanism under CD19 CAR-T failure: single-cell RNA sequencing of tumors at progression found clonally expanded, proliferating CART19 cells with transcriptional dysfunction features, reproduced that failure signature in vitro with reduced IL-2 signaling, and rescued it with the non-alpha IL-2 receptor agonist pegenzileukin — in a cell-line model from a post-CART19 progression tumor, CART19 alone gave a transient response then progression and secondary CNS relapse, while CART19 plus pegenzileukin eradicated the tumors. That is in vitro and a cell-line model, not patient data. And a retrospective single-centre study of 109 relapsed or refractory large B-cell lymphoma patients found comparable depth of response between nodal and extranodal disease (minus 62.1% versus 65.6%, then minus 77.9% versus 78.1%) alongside very different durability — median PFS 306.5 days nodal against 90 days extranodal — with pleural, hepatobiliary and adrenal involvement the worst-associated. The Vyriad item no longer claims to be the "only Western" in vivo lentiviral CAR-T or the only such study "outside China" (an Indonesia-site study is outside China), and the swept in vivo lentiviral study count is 20, not 16. The AbelZeta item no longer claims the "only registrational Phase 2" in lupus nephritis or the "broadest autoimmune CAR-T program" of any company reviewed, and no longer treats seven registry records as seven distinct active trials. The Oct 23 invikafusp item now separates registry sites from author affiliations: the registry lists 19 START-001 sites, including the National Institutes of Health in Bethesda and Massachusetts General Hospital Cancer Center in Boston, while Memorial Sloan Kettering and Mass General Brigham are author affiliations on the publication, not trial sites. And that item's disease-control figure — 59.3% among 27 patients at the optimal dose — has been withdrawn, because it traced only to a trade report that disallows automated access, and neither the Nature Medicine abstract nor Marengo's own release states it. The published figure is now the verifiable one: a 14.8% objective response rate (4/27) at the optimal biological doses, with confirmed partial responses in microsatellite-stable colorectal cancer and PD-L1-negative non-small cell lung cancer, traced to the PubMed abstract.
