Morning update — Oct 26 — TWO ESMO 2026 ITEMS, CHECKED AGAINST THE REGISTRY: the overnight scan added two more source-verified items, both conference readouts traced to primary registry records. Xencor picked 400 mcg/kg of XmAb819, an ENPP3 x CD3 T-cell engager, for advanced clear cell renal cell carcinoma — Phase 1 dose-expansion data presented at ESMO 2026 on October 26, 2026 by Dr. Sumanta Pal of City of Hope. The selected cohort reported a 45.5% confirmed objective response rate (95% CI 16.8–76.6), 55% unconfirmed ORR and an 82% 6-month Kaplan-Meier PFS estimate, with median PFS not reached and more than 70% still on treatment. Response rose with dose across expansion cohorts: 100 mcg/kg 30% cORR, 200 mcg/kg 44%, 400 mcg/kg 45.5%. The population was genuinely hard — median three prior lines, 94% intermediate- to poor-risk IMDC, every patient previously exposed to a PD-(L)1 inhibitor and a VEGFR TKI, 63% to two or more VEGFR TKIs, 34% to a HIF2-alpha inhibitor. The registry record matches: NCT05433142, a Phase 1 multiple-dose study sponsored by Xencor, recruiting, estimated enrollment 307, actual start June 13, 2022, estimated primary completion February 2027, completion December 2028, across 26 sites (20 United States, plus Belgium, France, Spain and the United Kingdom). The safety half of the story is the cautionary half: Grade 3 or higher related treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 72.1% of patients — AST increase 35.3%, ALT increase 33.8%, rash 22.1%, hypotension 7.4%, CRS 5.9% — with discontinuation for related events in 5.9%, though toxicity was front-loaded and after day 29 the Grade 3+ rates fell to ALT 10.3%, AST 8.8%, rash 2.9% and diarrhea 2.9%. One patient given roughly 10-fold intended exposure after a dose-preparation error developed Grade 4 CRS and recovered fully; all three such errors predated a new formulation. Correction of the competitive framing: Xencor calls XmAb819 a “potential first-in-class” ENPP3-targeted T-cell engager, but the registry also lists Janssen’s JNJ-87890387 (NCT06178614, Phase 1, recruiting, 200 patients, advanced solid tumors) and MD Anderson’s LNK001 (NCT07564232, Phase 1, recruiting, 40 patients, ccRCC) — so the claim is at best about the T-cell engager format, not about ENPP3 as a target. Xencor plans to discuss a potential accelerated approval pathway with FDA in standard-of-care-exhausted subsets, expects at least 20 evaluable patients at 400 mcg/kg to support dose selection in the first half of 2027, plans a registration-enabling study next year and a global randomized comparison against tivozanib, and targets registration-enabling development by end-2027. CARsgen moved satri-cel into the adjuvant setting — ESMO 2026 poster 1056P on October 26, 2026, reporting preliminary results from an investigator-initiated trial of the autologous Claudin18.2 CAR-T as consolidation therapy after adjuvant treatment in resected locally advanced gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer. At the September 14, 2026 cutoff, 10 patients had been infused, all pathologic stage T4a or T4b node-positive with up to 80% N2/N3 involvement (60% N3), 90% Lauren diffuse type and 70% signet-ring cell carcinoma; seven had completed at least 12 months of postoperative follow-up, five at 18 months and two at 21 months. None had recurred, the 12-month disease-free survival rate was 100% and median DFS was not reached, and one patient who was minimal residual disease-positive by circulating tumor DNA converted negative and stayed negative. CRS occurred in 80% of patients but all events were Grade 1–2 with a median four days to recovery, with no ICANS and no adverse events leading to death. The registry record is NCT06857786 (CT041-CG4010) — an open-label, multicenter Phase Ib consolidation study sponsored by Peking University, estimated enrollment 48 across 3 sites in China, estimated primary completion June 30, 2027 and completion June 30, 2030. The approval context matters more than the poster: satri-cel was approved by China’s NMPA in June 2026 for Claudin18.2-positive, HER2-negative advanced gastric or GEJ adenocarcinoma after at least two prior lines, which CARsgen describes as the world’s first approved CAR-T cell therapy for a solid tumor — an NMPA-specific, company-stated claim that does not conflict with Iovance’s lifileucel (amtagvi), a TIL rather than a CAR-T and FDA-approved in 2024 as the first cell therapy for a solid tumor. Caveats, stated plainly: the satri-cel dataset is ten patients, single-arm, with no comparator, in which a 100% 12-month DFS across seven evaluable patients is far too small to support an efficacy conclusion; and the trial record still reads NOT_YET_RECRUITING against a March 10, 2025 start date despite reported dosing of 10 patients, so the registry has not caught up with the company report. Late update — Oct 26 — TWO MORE SOURCE-VERIFIED ITEMS, AND A “TWO-HORSE RACE” FRAMING CORRECTED: the overnight pass adds two items and corrects one trade description. Kyverna’s miv-cel (mivocabtagene autoleucel, KYV-101) has held up in progressive multiple sclerosis — updated Phase 1 data presented at MS Toronto 2026 on October 23, 2026 by Dr. Jeffrey Dunn of Stanford: six patients dosed (33M n=3, 100M n=3) after bendamustine lymphodepletion, five with evaluable data, all five with stable or improved EDSS scores, 4/5 with sustained clinical benefit through one year, four patients with more than a year of follow-up sustained improvement without further need for chronic immunotherapies, and the first patient treated with sustained benefit beyond two years. CAR T-cell expansion was detected in peripheral blood with CSF penetration peaking around day 14, and complete CD19-positive B-cell depletion followed by naïve B-cell repopulation from month 3 — the “immune reset” the company describes. Safety was clean: no high-grade CRS, and no cases of ICANS or IEC-HS. The registry record matches the presentation: NCT06138132 is an open-label, single-center Phase 1 sponsored by Stanford University with Kyverna as collaborator, recruiting, estimated enrollment 12, actual start April 10, 2024, estimated primary completion June 2027, one site. Why this is the significant autoimmune CAR-T item this month: the field has been defined by safety failures — a fatal septic pneumonia 44 days after zola-cel infusion in the BMS Breakfree-2 study, and three fatal IEC-HS cases that paused Novartis’ rap-cel program — so a Phase 1 reporting no neurotoxicity is worth more than the efficacy numbers alone. miv-cel now holds three FDA RMAT designations (stiff person syndrome, generalized myasthenia gravis, non-active secondary progressive MS) with a development update in progressive MS due by early 2027; the registrational myasthenia gravis study is KYSA-6 (NCT06193889, 66 patients, 22 sites across six countries, recruiting) and the progressive-MS registrational study is KYSA-7 (NCT06384976, 120 patients, active not recruiting). Caveats, stated plainly: five evaluable patients, single-arm, single-center, company-reported rather than peer-reviewed, EDSS is a coarse ordinal scale, and the two-year durability claim rests on one patient. Two Rett syndrome gene therapies are approaching the FDA — Taysha’s TSHA-102 and Neurogene’s NGN-401, both single intrathecal AAV doses for a disorder with no gene therapy option. The registry puts Taysha’s REVEAL study at NCT05606614 (Phase 1/2/3, actual enrollment 17, active not recruiting, estimated primary completion June 2031, 9 sites in the United States and Canada) and Neurogene’s Embolden study at NCT05898620 (Phase 3, estimated enrollment 33, active not recruiting, estimated primary completion December 2029, 16 sites across the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia). Correction — the “two-horse race” framing is too narrow. ARPA-H states the cold chain "adds tens of billions of dollars in cost every year" and that a dose worth hundreds of thousands of dollars "can be ruined from just one power outage." Strategically the most underrated item this cycle: if therapies move at room temperature, infusion-site economics invert, the distribution model changes, and value shifts from the per-patient cell to a campaign-manufactured stabilized product — a direct subtraction from autologous CDMO slot utilization. Research funding, not demonstrated capability. Correction 1 — the "world's first" in vivo CAR-T autoimmune claim is false. At least seventeen in vivo CAR-T studies in autoimmune indications are already registered, and the earliest of them predate BMS by more than a year. The first is JY231 (NCT06887985, estimated start March 31, 2025); others include an LNP-mRNA in vivo CAR-T (NCT07362758, start January 2026), an in vivo CAR-T in refractory Graves' disease (NCT07333677, start January 2026), a circular-RNA in vivo CAR-T (NCT07413341) and a lentiviral in vivo CAR-T in refractory autoimmune disease (NCT07490951, start March 2026). Two earlier items in this bulletin are withdrawn on this point: OL-CD19-GDT (NCT07167537) was cited as an in vivo autoimmune CAR-T, but its own registry title says allogeneic CAR-T — it is an ex vivo cell product, not an in vivo one; and a “CD8-targeted LNP CD19 CAR mRNA study in five refractory SLE patients” was cited without a registry record, and no such record could be located in a targeted sweep, so it is not traceable and has been removed. Correction 2 — "first big pharma" also needs narrowing. AbbVie already has an in vivo CAR-T autoimmune trial running through its $2.1B acquisition of Capstan: NCT06917742, CPTX2309 in healthy volunteers and in moderate-to-severe RA and SLE, recruiting since April 2025. The accurate framing is that BMS-986553 is the first large-cap pharma to run a dedicated in vivo CAR-T autoimmune Phase 1 as a sponsored, named commercial product — a real distinction, but not "world's first." Two further items landed this cycle. Vyriad opened a US Phase 1 of an in vivo BCMA CAR-T delivered by a T-cell-targeted lentiviral vector (NCT07802717, 40 patients, recruiting at Mayo Clinic). A registry sweep finds 31 in vivo lentiviral CAR-T studies worldwide — 27 China-linked, and four outside China: Kite/Gilead’s INT2104 (Australia and Spain sites, actual start September 20, 2024), Kelonia’s KLN-1010 (eight US sites, actual start July 16, 2025), this Vyriad study (US, September 2026), and LV009 (Indonesia). This is therefore not the only such study with a US sponsor and US sites, and not the first. AbelZeta has a company-described registrational Phase 2 in lupus nephritis for an autologous CAR-T (NCT07729826, FDA RMAT clearance, 50 patients, 104-week follow-up, Complete Renal Response as the primary endpoint), also holding EMA PRIME, across seven registry records for the same bispecific product, though the records do not correspond to seven distinct active trials — one is withdrawn and several are early-phase or exploratory — and including a 119-patient Phase 1b/2 in progressive MS. Two more items landed in the evening refresh. Prime Medicine dosed the first patient with in-body prime editing — PM577a, an LNP-delivered prime editor for Wilson disease targeting the p.H1069Q ATP7B mutation, is the first reported in vivo prime-editing therapy dosed in a person. No cell extraction, no ex vivo editing, no reinfusion; NCT07748403, 42 participants, recruiting. No safety or efficacy data yet. Marengo's invikafusp alfa read out eight tumor types at ESMO 2026 — 62 evaluable TMB-H solid tumor patients, 56% tumor shrinkage, 18% ORR, 60% DCR, no ICANS and no Grade 5 treatment-related adverse events in 104 safety patients — and the FDA granted a second Fast Track expanding it to TMB-H solid tumors regardless of tissue of origin. It is not a cell therapy product, which is exactly why it belongs here: a non-cell-based way of engaging T cells, with no harvest, no manufacturing slot and no autologous product to ship. Nepal treated its first patient with locally manufactured CAR-T, with Dr. Luca Gattinoni (LIT- Leibniz) framing it as a model for low- and middle-income countries — a direct challenge to centralized-manufacturing economics. Charles River, Labcorp and the FDA signed a research collaboration to validate standardized preclinical abuse-potential assessment methods — a preclinical and regulatory-science item, labeled as such. Two more items landed in the final scan, both peer-reviewed, and both correct an earlier read of them as commentary rather than primary research. A Blood paper from MD Anderson with Sanofi co-authors put a mechanism under CD19 CAR-T failure: single-cell RNA sequencing of tumors at progression found clonally expanded, proliferating CART19 cells with transcriptional dysfunction features, reproduced that failure signature in vitro with reduced IL-2 signaling, and rescued it with the non-alpha IL-2 receptor agonist pegenzileukin — in a cell-line model from a post-CART19 progression tumor, CART19 alone gave a transient response then progression and secondary CNS relapse, while CART19 plus pegenzileukin eradicated the tumors. That is in vitro and a cell-line model, not patient data. And a retrospective single-centre study of 109 relapsed or refractory large B-cell lymphoma patients found comparable depth of response between nodal and extranodal disease (minus 62.1% versus 65.6%, then minus 77.9% versus 78.1%) alongside very different durability — median PFS 306.5 days nodal against 90 days extranodal — with pleural, hepatobiliary and adrenal involvement the worst-associated. All carry verified clickable original links, including ClinicalTrials.gov records. Two further items landed in the final scan, and both are in Duchenne. Regenxbio’s RGX-202 met its Phase 3 primary endpoint in the AFFINITY DUCHENNE trial (NCT05693142): mean microdystrophin expression above 10% of normal at week 12, presented at the Child Neurology Society meeting on October 14–17, 2026, with biopsies in 30 participants and 93% of them individually above the threshold, and week-12 expression correlating with one-year North Star Ambulatory Assessment change (P = .0002). Two drug-related serious adverse events — myocarditis in an 8-year-old and liver injury in a 10-year-old — both resolved without lasting effects. The caveats matter more than the headline: open-label, single-arm, functional findings resting on nine boys at one year against weighted external comparison groups rather than a placebo arm, presented at a meeting and not yet in a full peer-reviewed paper, and as of October 20 no public announcement confirmed the BLA filing had started against a stated Q3 2026 plan for accelerated approval. A locked registry sweep of Duchenne-condition interventional gene-therapy records puts 6 Phase 3 programmes still active across 5 assets — RGX-202, Pfizer's PF-06939926, Sarepta's delandistrogene moxeparvovec, Solid's SGT-003 and Genethon's GNT0004 — while Elevidys, the one approved product, is under commercial pressure with Sarepta's first-quarter 2026 gene therapy revenue at $331 million. And the FDA is writing the rules that will decide which of these packages get approved. Its “Fit-for-Purpose Rare Disease Framework Initiative” public meeting is set for October 28, 2026, with comments due December 11, convened by the Reagan-Udall Foundation with the FDA. The scope is the development and regulatory review of rare disease therapies — which is to say surrogate endpoints, external control groups and single-arm designs without a concurrent placebo comparator, the exact three grounds on which RGX-121 was rejected in February. Regenxbio’s chief executive said in May that the new leadership would have a mandate on rare disease flexibility, and the June reversal on NAVSUNLI is the first observable evidence of it.
