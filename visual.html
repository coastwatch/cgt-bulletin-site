Ex vivo to in vivo — vector becomes the drug: scale, purity, cell line and analytics all shift

Ex vivo to in vivo: shaping the next generation of viral vector manufacturing — BioSpace feature in partnership with AGC Biologics, August 10, 2026. Paradigm shift: ex vivo — vector is a manufacturing tool, drug product is the engineered cell, impurities mitigated downstream; in vivo — vector IS the drug, no further purification opportunity, far stricter purity, far less regulatory flexibility (endotoxin, mycoplasma, residual DNA, HCP held to injectable-biologic standards). Regulators: EMA typically classifies ex vivo vectors as starting materials; FDA treats as drug substances but permits risk-based approach since the final product is the cell. Scale: ex vivo low dose, in vivo orders of magnitude more → 200 L → 2,000 L bioreactors; dedicated transfection-complex mixing procedures; downstream re-optimization (columns/membranes don't scale linearly); equipment layout + process flow planned 6–12 months ahead. Cell line bottleneck shifts from efficiency to regulatory risk: HEK293T expresses SV40 large T antigen (oncoprotein, interferes with p53/RB) — mitigable downstream in ex vivo, a live safety concern in vivo where trace residual LTA DNA/protein may matter. Sponsored feature — reflects AGC's commercial interests.
