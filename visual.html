EBMT CAR-T CRYOPRESERVATION — FREEZE ≤48 H AFTER MANUFACTURING, INITIATE ≤30 MIN AFTER CRYOMEDIUM ADDITION, CONTROLLED-RATE TO ≥−80 °C, LONG-TERM <−120 °C; RETENTION SAMPLES + POST-THAW VIABILITY + IDENTITY TESTING + VALIDATED TRANSPORT MANDATORY; 52-CENTRE SURVEY SHOWED WIDE VARIABILITY; −80 °C PASSIVE FREEZING ACCEPTABLE AS BACK-UP WITH LOCAL VALIDATION

The European Bone Marrow Transplantation group’s practice harmonisation and guidelines committee published consensus recommendations in Bone Marrow Transplantation (November 9, 2026) on cryopreservation of academic CAR T-cell products — a methodology paper for centres that manufacture CAR T-cells in-house rather than under a licence. The motivation is stated plainly: academic CAR T-cell manufacturing is expanding rapidly across Europe while cryopreservation practice remains heterogeneous, which raises concerns about product comparability. An EBMT survey of 52 academic centres found wide variability in freezing timing, cryomedium composition, device choice, storage temperature and post-thaw quality control, and no harmonised guidance existed for CAR T-cell products. The recommendations themselves are the usable output: perform cryopreservation as soon as possible after manufacturing, preferably within 48 hours; begin freezing within 30 minutes of cryomedium addition; use controlled-rate freezing to at least −80 °C, with long-term storage below −120 °C wherever feasible; and treat retention samples, post-thaw viability, identity testing and validated transportation procedures as mandatory. Liquid nitrogen and Peltier-based controlled-rate freezing systems were regarded as technically equivalent, and −80 °C passive freezing — the workhorse method in most academic centres — was noted as successfully used and acceptable as a back-up method where local confirmation of suitability is provided through formal method validation. Why it belongs in a manufacturing-facing bulletin: this is the first harmonised reference for how an academic or hospital cell-therapy unit should freeze and store the product it manufactures itself. For centres without a licence agreement and a validated commercial freeze protocol, it supplies the technical specification — timing, temperature, device equivalence and the mandatory post-thaw and transport checks — rather than leaving each site to invent its own. It also makes the comparability problem explicit: if freezing practice varies across sites, differences in product characteristics cannot be attributed to the product alone. Scope: the recommendations are consensus-based technical guidance for groups establishing academic CAR T-cell manufacturing, written by a 12-author expert panel after an EBMT workshop using the society’s harmonisation methodology, drawing on survey data, literature and professional experience. What the Federal Register notice states about the final guidance: a potency assurance strategy is a multifaceted approach that reduces risks to the potency of a product through (1) manufacturing process design, (2) manufacturing process control, (3) material control, (4) in-process testing, and (5) potency lot release assays — the goal being to ensure that every lot released has the specific ability or capacity to achieve the intended therapeutic effect. Ex vivo to in vivo — vector becomes the drug: scale, purity, cell line and analytics all shift

Ex vivo to in vivo: shaping the next generation of viral vector manufacturing — BioSpace feature in partnership with AGC Biologics, August 10, 2026.
