Cell & Gene Therapy Industry Bulletin

A combined market bulletin on the most important current developments in cell and gene therapy, with emphasis on strategy, regulatory tone, manufacturing, financing, and competitive implications.

Executive Summary

Cell and gene therapy remains strategically important, but 2026 news flow shows a market that is maturing under pressure rather than simply expanding. The clearest signals are that big pharma is still committing capital, regulatory unpredictability remains one of the biggest sector constraints, manufacturing execution is increasingly a board-level issue, China is becoming more central to both asset creation and CGT manufacturing strategy, and commercial proof exists but adoption remains operationally difficult.

Bottom line: the sector is not retreating strategically, but the bar for winning has moved from scientific novelty to evidence quality + manufacturability + execution + commercial practicality.

Top Industry Themes

1. Execution beats novelty

The market now rewards programs with clearer development logic, stronger CMC, and a believable commercialization path—not just platform excitement.

2. FDA remains a swing factor

The approval of Rocket's Kresladi contrasts with Regenxbio's rejection, reinforcing how uneven regulatory outcomes can materially reshape sentiment.

3. Pharma still wants in

Major transactions and internal buildouts show continued strategic conviction in cell therapy and genetic medicine, especially for differentiated assets.

4. China matters more

China is increasingly relevant as a source of assets, manufacturing capability, strategic partnerships, and pricing/competitive pressure.

Manufacturing: The Central Pressure Point

Across Endpoints, GlobalData context, and open reporting, manufacturing appears repeatedly as a cause of:

  • approval delays
  • investor concern
  • supply interruptions
  • longer time-to-revenue
  • higher perceived execution risk

The sector increasingly values partners and internal teams that can reduce CMC uncertainty, support comparability, improve transfer reliability, and make commercialization more executable.

What This Means for the Market

  • Cell therapy sentiment is stronger than broad gene therapy sentiment, especially where evidence packages are more robust and commercial pathways look clearer.
  • High-quality assets can still command large deals, but weak or operationally messy stories are punished quickly.
  • Manufacturing capability is becoming a source of strategic differentiation, not just a background service function.
  • China is now both an opportunity and a competitive threat in high-value CGT categories.
  • Commercial scale-up remains hard, even for highly visible approved therapies.

Integrated View

The current CGT market is very much alive, but it has entered a harder, more disciplined phase. Large pharma is still investing, approvals still happen, and differentiated assets still attract premium valuations. At the same time, the industry is increasingly unforgiving of weak evidence, manufacturing problems, and commercialization friction. The companies—and partners—that win next will be the ones that make advanced therapies not just scientifically compelling, but operationally executable.

🔬 Endpoints News🌐 Open WebOctober 23, 2026
Patient death in Bristol Myers Squibb autoimmune CAR-T trial — zola-cel, fatal septic pneumonia 44 days post-infusion

This is the most consequential safety development in the sector this cycle and should be treated as the lead item. BMS disclosed that a patient with highly active relapsing multiple sclerosis died after receiving zolacabtagene autoleucel (zola-cel) in the Phase 1 Breakfree-2 autoimmune study. The sequence reported by BMS: after infusion the patient developed late-onset, non-immune ICANS motor symptoms that could not be resolved with intermittent corticosteroids and IVIG; the patient then developed septic pneumonia, died the day after, and 44 days after the infusion. No additional deaths have been reported across the zola-cel autoimmune programs, which comprise 190 Phase 1 patients treated. Breakfree-2 (66 patients across MS and refractory myasthenia gravis) showed CRS in 42.4% of participants (28/66), all Grade 1 or 2 and resolving in a median of two days, plus one other life-threatening ICANS event that resolved with corticosteroids and anakinra within four days. The primary endpoint was safety; efficacy was secondary, with BMS reporting early promising efficacy and all dose-level-2 patients off disease-modifying therapy, nine followed over 12 months. Why this matters beyond BMS: this death follows Novartis' rap-cel autoimmune trial pause in September after three fatal IEC-HS cases, so two of the largest cell therapy programs now have a death signal in autoimmune disease specifically. The benefit-risk question for CAR T in non-fatal conditions is now a live sector-wide debate, and autoimmune is precisely the indication where CDMO demand projections have been most aggressive. Note also that BMS voluntarily paused some autoimmune zola-cel trials in late August without disclosing the safety events, and that William Blair had flagged at least one serious IEC-HS case in zola-cel at that time.

Phase 1/2 trial of bispecific dual T-cell agonist invikafusp alfa halts progression in checkpoint-inhibitor-resistant solid tumors

The NIH/Mass General Brigham/Memorial Sloan Kettering phase 1/2 START-001 trial of invikafusp alfa (invika), published in Nature Medicine, reports that among 27 patients treated at the optimal dose, disease did not progress in 16 (59.3%) and four (14.8%) had greater than 30% tumor shrinkage — two with metastatic colorectal cancer on seventh-line therapy, one with stage 4 NSCLC. The mechanism is selective: invika engages only two specific tumor-infiltrating T-cell populations rather than activating T cells broadly, which is the design intent of avoiding the severe side effects of less selective T-cell agonists. Marengo Therapeutics is the sponsor and holds FDA Fast Track for invika in antigen-rich metastatic colorectal cancer and in similar post-checkpoint-inhibitor solid tumors. Caveats worth stating: early-phase data in heterogeneous, heavily pretreated patients, and the authors themselves note immune activity did not always translate into clinical response. This is a precision-oncology modality rather than a cell therapy product, but it is directly relevant to the sector's competitive boundary — engineered, non-cell-based T-cell engagement is a substitution risk for cell therapy development pipelines.

🌐 Conference coverageOctober 24, 2026
Lifileucel TIL therapy moved to first line: 29.2% complete responses with pembrolizumab in advanced melanoma at ESMO

The Phase 2 IOV-COM-202 trial, conducted at The Royal Marsden NHS Foundation Trust and presented at ESMO 2026 by Dr. Andrew Furness, tested moving lifileucel (TIL therapy) earlier in the treatment pathway rather than after checkpoint inhibitor failure. In 24 evaluable patients with ICI-naïve advanced melanoma, 15 (62.5%) had significant tumor shrinkage and 7 had complete responses (29.2%), with median duration of response not reached at a median follow-up of 26.9 months, 13 of the 15 responders maintaining response at least 12 months, median PFS of 19.8 months, and median OS not reached at 50.9 months of follow-up. No additional side effects beyond those of the individual components were observed. Lifileucel (amtagvi) became the first FDA-approved cell therapy for a solid tumor in 2024, on a 31.4% response rate in post-progression patients; this dataset, if confirmed, would roughly double that benchmark by moving earlier. The trial is funded by Iovance Biotherapeutics. The randomized Phase 3 TILVANCE-301 (lifileucel plus pembrolizumab vs pembrolizumab alone) is ongoing with Furness as UK National Chief Investigator. Caveats: 24 evaluable patients is a small single-arm dataset with the usual early-line immortal-time and selection biases, and Iovance funded the study, so the Phase 3 read-out is what matters. For manufacturing: first-line positioning would materially expand eligible patient volume for TIL production, and TIL is among the most logistically demanding cell products — but the IOV-COM-202 result also underlines that TIL access is currently limited to specialist centers.

McKesson/InspiroGene 2026 CGT Report: only ~2 in 10 CAR-T-eligible patients receive treatment; rheumatologists only "minimally to moderately" prepared for 77 late-stage autoimmune therapies

InspiroGene by McKesson published its third annual 2026 Cell and Gene Therapy Report, drawing on primary research with 200 US physicians, interviews with 35 payer leaders covering approximately 295 million covered lives, late-stage US CGT pipeline analysis, and patient/advocate input. Several findings are directly material to the commercial and manufacturing outlook. Access gap: only about two in ten patients eligible for CAR-T treatment actually receive it, with patient out-of-pocket costs (notably travel and lodging) and coverage delays among the most cited reasons referred patients never get treated — a distributed-manufacturing and site-of-care economics problem, not just a manufacturing one. Adoption is growing: the average number of patients oncologists treat with CGTs has nearly doubled since 2024, from 17 to 32 annually, with 97% saying CGTs could offer some patients the best chance of a cure. Autoimmune readiness is the weak link: rheumatologists describe themselves as only "minimally" to "moderately" prepared for the 77 autoimmune disease therapies in the late-stage pipeline, and while most have begun taking steps to prepare, many want more safety and efficacy and real-world evidence first — a direct read on the commercial ramp for the autoimmune segment that also frames the recent zola-cel and rap-cel safety events. Care delivery structure is contested: 65% of oncologists expect CGT care to stay centralized at oncologist-led treatment centers, while 64% of rheumatologists and 62% of ophthalmologists expect decentralization into their own specialties. Payer mechanics differ by modality: cell therapy contracting is shifting toward contracted case rates, while gene therapy remains dominated by single-case agreements given lower volumes and greater cost scrutiny. More than 2,000 US sites now conduct CGT clinical trials. Note the report is a McKesson publication, so treat it as a market-research perspective rather than neutral evidence, but it is a useful independent read on where the real bottleneck sits.
