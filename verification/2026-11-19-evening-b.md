### 1. A Nature Medicine paper identifies the driver of the gut complication after BCMA CAR-T

- **Primary source:** [Ahmed S, Ayers AA, Cortina F, et al., "Expansion and cytotoxicity of mucosal CAR+ T cells in enterocolitis associated with BCMA CAR-T cell therapy," *Nature Medicine*, published November 19, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04731-w) — doi 10.1038/s41591-026-04731-w, volume 1, first page 1. The paper page was fetched and the abstract read in full. The paper is not yet indexed in PubMed.
- **Institutional release, read for the clinical framing:** [UT MD Anderson, "Researchers uncover cause of serious digestive complication after CAR T cell therapy in patients with multiple myeloma"](https://www.mdanderson.org/newsroom/researchers-uncover-cause-of-serious-digestive-complication-after-CAR-T-cell-therapy-in-patients-with-multiple-myeloma.h00-159860079.html) — HTTP 200, body read directly. This release is what links the paper, and the corresponding author is **Dr. Mark Hamilton**, assistant professor of Lymphoma & Myeloma at MD Anderson.
- **The finding:** **immune effector cell-associated enterocolitis (IEC-EC)** after CAR T-cell therapy is driven by **persistent, clonal CAR-positive T cells infiltrating the gastrointestinal tract**. The authors' framing is the one the item carries: IEC-EC pathology represents **a distinct, duodenum-predominant, clonal lymphoproliferative disorder**, not general intestinal inflammation.
- **Population, with denominators:** nine cases of cilta-cel-associated IEC-EC with CAR-positive infiltration among **158 cilta-cel-treated patients** at MD Anderson — an incidence of **5.1% at a six-month landmark and 5.7% globally**.
- **Mechanism, from the paper's own data:** cilta-cel infiltration was the primary cause of IEC-EC, and **all cases demonstrated monoclonal or oligoclonal T-cell populations** as measured by vector integration analysis, flow cytometry and T-cell receptor sequencing. Patients with IEC-EC had **100-fold more CAR T-cell persistence in peripheral blood** than patients without it. RNA sequencing showed **increased cytotoxic and inflammatory programs** in the CAR-positive infiltrates relative to controls.
- **The distinction the paper draws itself, and the item preserves:** clonal vector integration occurred in introns of highly expressed genes **without evidence of vector-mediated mutagenesis**, and candidate driver mutations were **sparse and heterogeneous** — which the authors say distinguishes IEC-EC from the peripheral T-cell lymphoma that can follow CAR T-cell therapy. The item reports both halves of that sentence, because a reader who heard only "clonal" would draw the wrong conclusion.
- **What the authors propose, and the limit they set:** routine monitoring of CAR T-cell persistence could help identify patients at elevated risk, and duodenal endoscopy with tissue testing for CAR T cells could accelerate diagnosis and targeted therapy. The investigators state that further studies are needed to determine which patients are most prone to the syndrome and to establish optimal prevention protocols. **The item states that the prevention protocol the authors call for does not yet exist.**
- **Funding and collaboration:** the study was conducted in collaboration with the **U.S. Multiple Myeloma Immunotherapy Consortium** and funded by the UT MD Anderson Lymphoid Malignancies Program and the Paula and Rodger Riney Foundation.
- **Scope note:** the safety signal is specific to BCMA-directed CAR T-cell therapy in myeloma, and the mechanism is a diagnostic and monitoring proposition rather than a change to the product.
- **Duplicate check:** the site had **zero** prior mentions of enterocolitis, IEC-EC, cilta-cel or ciltacabtagene on any of the five pages.

### 2. First-in-human report of a lymphodepletion-free off-the-shelf therapy for refractory lupus nephritis

- **Primary source:** ["Allogeneic trifunctional NK cell therapy combined with rituximab for refractory mixed class IV/V lupus nephritis without lymphodepletion," *Cellular & Molecular Immunology*, published November 19, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41423-026-01489-6) — doi 10.1038/s41423-026-01489-6. The paper page was fetched and the abstract read in full. Not yet indexed in PubMed.
- **The clinical problem the design targets:** refractory lupus nephritis, particularly **mixed class IV plus V histology**, is the most treatment-resistant subtype; rituximab often gives incomplete B-cell depletion and limited efficacy in refractory patients, while CAR T-cell therapy carries substantial risks from lymphodepletion. The approach is built to avoid both problems.
- **The construct:** NK cells engineered to express **NKG2D** (targeting stress ligands on autoimmune B cells), **high levels of CD16** (for enhanced antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity with rituximab), and **membrane-bound IL-15 and IL-15R alpha** (for in-vivo survival).
- **Preclinical:** in vitro, the cells selectively killed SLE patient B cells via NKG2D-MICA/B recognition and enhanced ADCC. In NOG mice, the engineered trifunctional NK cells persisted for up to **90 days**, with a **30-fold greater peak abundance** than unmodified NK cells, and the combination was preliminarily validated in a human SLE-PBMC xenograft model.
- **Clinical result, in two patients:** in a first-in-human trial, **two patients** with refractory mixed-class lupus nephritis (IV-G(A/C) + V and IV-G(A) + V), massive proteinuria, hematuria and multiorgan involvement, whose intensive immunosuppression had failed, were treated. The therapy was **well tolerated, causing no cytokine release syndrome and no neurotoxicity**. Both patients achieved **rapid B-cell depletion**, with **proteinuria reductions of 73% and 52% respectively**, and **sustained SRI-4 responses** indicating meaningful clinical improvement while being maintained on low-dose steroids. **The item keeps the denominator visible: this is two patients.**
- **Registry (v2 API; robots.txt read first this session):** **NCT06676631** is a **recruiting** Phase 1 study of NK010 or NK042 cell injection with rituximab in refractory SLE or lupus nephritis, **18 participants estimated**, sponsor **Guangdong Provincial People's Hospital**, actual start **February 19, 2025**, estimated primary completion **December 31, 2026**, estimated completion December 31, 2027, last update posted **June 12, 2026**, **one site in China**, with NK010 given by dose escalation at 2.5, 5.0 and 7.5 × 10⁹ NK cells and rituximab 1 g infused over 60 minutes. **Two discrepancies the item flags:** the registry still describes NK010 as "an allogeneic **non-genetically modified** NK cell infusion" while the paper describes genetically engineered cells, and the record has not been updated since June 2026.
- **Scope note:** no lupus nephritis cell or gene therapy appears on FDA's approved cellular and gene therapy products list, and this is a first-in-human result in two patients at a single site.
- **Duplicate check:** the site had **zero** prior mentions of trifunctional, NK010, NKX010 or this programme.

### 3. The UK modernises the legal definition of gene therapy medicinal products

- **Primary source:** [GOV.UK / MHRA, "Government backs modernisation of gene therapy definitions," published 19 November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-backs-modernisation-of-gene-therapy-definitions) — HTTP 200, body read directly. The release is from the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency.
- **What was decided:** the government confirmed plans to **modernise the legal definition of gene therapy medicinal products (GTMPs)**, so that **certain synthetic gene therapies and novel genome-editing treatments are regulated under the UK's established advanced therapies framework** rather than sitting outside it.
- **The route:** the proposals were published in the government's response to a consultation led by the **MHRA and the Department of Health in Northern Ireland**, and **received support from 83% of respondents**. Subject to international notification procedures and parliamentary approval, they will **amend the Human Medicines Regulations 2012** to reflect advances in synthetic genetic material and genome-editing technologies.
- **What does not change:** the existing approval process and the standards for safety, quality and efficacy will remain unchanged, and **medicines already licensed will not need to be reclassified**. The stated rationale is that developers get greater certainty about how their medicines are classified and the requirements they must meet, so they can plan with confidence.
- **Where it was announced:** the inaugural **DIA-MHRA Global Summit**, held in London on 16–17 November 2026 as part of London Life Sciences Week, hosted jointly by the MHRA and the Drug Information Association.
- **Status, stated precisely in the item:** this is a government decision to amend regulations, **contingent on international notification and parliamentary approval** — it is not yet a change in law, and it does not settle the classification of any individual product.
- **Duplicate check:** the site carries two prior UK MHRA items — the Casgevy age expansion (`pub-ind-mhra-casgevy-age5`) and the Ultragenyx UX111 marketing authorisation application (`pub-ind-ultragenyx-ux111-uk-maa`) — but **zero** prior mentions of GTMP, the Human Medicines Regulations 2012 or a gene therapy definition change. This is a different subject.
- **Scope note:** this is a regulatory-classification change rather than a manufacturing change, but it decides which products sit inside the advanced therapies licensing and GMP machinery.
