### 2. Lineage acquires NeuroAirmid and the Huntington's cell therapy HUNT1

- **Primary source:** [SEC EDGAR — Lineage 8-K, November 12, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/876343/000119312526458377/lctx-20261112.htm) — HTTP 200 with a declared User-Agent, body read directly, including **Item 1.01** (the Stock Purchase Agreement), Item 2.01, Item 2.02 and Item 7.01. Deal terms also in [Exhibit 99.2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/876343/000119312526458377/lctx-ex99_2.htm) and Q3 results in Exhibit 99.1.
- **The transaction:** Lineage acquired all outstanding shares of NeuroAirmid Therapeutics, which **became a wholly owned subsidiary**, and with it **HUNT1 (hNSC-01)**, a pluripotent stem cell-derived neural cell therapy for Huntington's disease now in a Phase 1b/2a study, REGEN4HD. The announcement was made on the same day the acquisition closed.
- **Consideration, from Item 1.01 rather than the press release:** at closing Lineage **(i) paid $2.4 million in cash to the sellers, (ii) issued 2.2 million common shares** — calculated by dividing $2.1 million by the 20-day volume-weighted average price ending two days before closing — **(iii) paid $0.2 million into an expense fund, and (iv) paid an aggregate $1.7 million to satisfy NeuroAirmid's obligations to third parties.** Lineage also agreed to pay **up to $5.4 million on the first anniversary of closing and up to $48.0 million in success-based milestones**. The press release's headline is "$4.7 million in cash and equity at closing and up to $5 million on the first anniversary"; the 8-K's own arithmetic is $2.4M cash + $2.1M of shares ($0.2M + $1.7M being separate expense-fund and third-party payments), and the anniversary amount is $5.4 million in the 8-K. **The item carries the 8-K figures.**
- **The licence:** NeuroAirmid entered into an **Amended and Restated Exclusive License Agreement with The Regents of the University of California** for the pluripotent stem cell-derived programme. Lineage assumes responsibility for clinical development of HUNT1 after REGEN4HD completes, and for all future cell manufacturing, process development, scale-up and optimisation, potentially using its AlloSCOPE platform.
- **The trial:** REGEN4HD is a Phase 1b/2a study of a **single dose, one-time administration** of hNSC-01, up to 21 patients across 5 planned cohorts. Phase 1b enrols up to 12 patients across 4 cohorts (A–D) at **2, 4, 8 and 16 million cells**; Phase 2a enrols up to 9 patients at a maximum tolerated dose set from Phase 1b. The first three Cohort A patients receive **unilateral** administration with an option for a similar transplant following DSMB approval. Primary safety outcome is treatment-related AEs at 6 and 12 weeks; secondary outcomes at 12 months include Total Motor Score, Total Functioning Capacity, functional and structural MRI, CSF and plasma neurofilament light chain, cognitive testing and behaviour.
- **The funding and the obligation Lineage took on:** the HUNT1 programme has advanced on **$16 million of translational funding from CIRM**, with REGEN4HD supported by a CIRM award of **up to $12 million issued in April 2026** — and **Lineage is responsible for paying up to $3.1 million over the grant term, expected through February 2030, replacing CIRM's own co-funding arrangement.** This is the clause a CDMO reader should note: the acquirer inherited the funding obligation along with the asset.
- **Registry (v2 API):** **NCT07451613** (REGEN4HD) is a **recruiting** Phase 1/2 study of human neural stem cells for Huntington's disease, **21 participants estimated**, sponsor **Leslie Thompson**, actual start April 6, 2026, estimated primary completion June 2028, estimated completion January 2031, last update posted **April 13, 2026**, **one US site**, intervention hNSC-01, acronym REGEN4HD, org study id ASCC-26-01. **The item flags both oddities:** the registry sponsor is an individual rather than Lineage because the record predates the acquisition, and the record was last updated six months before the announcement. A sponsor sweep for Lineage returns five records, **none of them REGEN4HD**.
- **Rest of the business:** the Phase 2a GAlette study of OpRegen for dry AMD with geographic atrophy, run by Genentech, is open and active at **17 sites** in the US and Israel; a US patent was issued covering the AlloSCOPE "5D" technology; the wholly owned COR1 corneal endothelial programme entered preclinical testing with initial data expected Q1 2027; the William Demant Invest ReSonance collaboration advanced into a second year with a Danish Medicines Agency meeting scheduled in Q4 2026. Cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities **$45.2 million** at September 30, 2026, expected to support operations into Q2 2028; total revenues approximately $1.5 million, a net decrease of $2.2 million.

### 3. Andelyn will develop and manufacture CLZ002 for Celliaz

- **Primary source:** [Andelyn Biosciences — partnership announcement, November 12, 2026](https://andelyn.bio/news-media/andelyn-to-manufacture-gene-therapy-for-celliaz) — HTTP 200, body read directly, dateline **COLUMBUS, Ohio, November 12, 2026**. The announcement is published by Andelyn; **Celliaz's own newsroom carries no release for it**.
- **The deal:** Andelyn, a cell and gene therapy CDMO, will provide **end-to-end development and manufacturing** for **CLZ002**, an AAV-based gene therapy for retinitis pigmentosa, using its **Curator Suspension Platform for AAV2 vector and plasmid manufacturing**, to support **IND-enabling studies and a planned Phase 1/2 trial**.
- **Mechanism, as the companies describe it:** Celliaz's approach is **blocking PROX1**, which suppresses the regenerative activity of Müller glia after transferring into Müller glia from neighbouring retinal cells. The therapy uses an **AAV2 capsid**. Celliaz's CEO describes CLZ002 as **gene-agnostic** rather than mutation-specific — RP can result from mutations in more than 90 genes — with the aim of restoring the retina's intrinsic regenerative capacity by preventing PROX1 transfer into Müller glia, which the company says retain latent regenerative potential.
- **What is not yet true, stated plainly in the item:** a registry search for **CLZ002**, for **Celliaz as sponsor**, and for **PROX1 in retinitis pigmentosa** returns **no matching study** — the only nearby hit is an unrelated clozapine sample-collection study. **No trial is registered, no IND has been cleared, and no patient has been dosed.** The release itself describes the Phase 1/2 as *planned*.
- **The CDMO facts, which are the substance for this bulletin's readers:** Andelyn describes itself as a full-service, **FDA-inspected** cell and gene therapy commercial CDMO with more than 20 years of experience, material for more than **500 cGMP batches and 85 global clinical trials, including an FDA-approved AAV gene therapy**, operating from Columbus, Ohio, covering plasmid engineering and manufacturing, process and analytical development, and cGMP clinical and commercial manufacturing. Its Chief Commercial Officer says the relationship "adds to our work supporting multiple other South Korean programs" — a portfolio statement, not an exclusivity claim.
- **Scope note:** no retinitis pigmentosa product appears on FDA's approved cellular and gene therapy products list — fetched and read this session.

### 4. First-in-human placenta-derived stem cells healed 10 of 10 diabetic foot ulcers

- **Primary source:** [Carstens MH, Ríos AB, Sevilla J, et al., "Treatment of diabetic foot chronic ulcers and neuropathy with placenta-derived stem cells," *Stem Cells Translational Medicine*, published online October 16, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42976200/) — 15(11):szag085, doi 10.1093/stcltm/szag085, PMID 42976200, PMCID PMC13730048. Abstract read in full via eutils; it is open access. *Stem Cells Translational Medicine* returns 203 to plain curl, which is its known bot guard, not a dead link.
- **Trial design:** subjects had non-healing wounds of **10 to 100 cm²** despite standard therapy for three or more months. Ten patients received placental-derived stem cell implantation **to the ulcer circumference, into the ulcer bed and along the pedal arteries**, with total dose escalation of **30, 40 and 60 million cells in 4, 3 and 3 subjects** respectively.
- **Results:** **no PSC-treatment-related serious adverse events**; wound healing demonstrated in **10/10 subjects at 6 months**; diabetic peripheral neuropathy improved in all subjects, with **9 of 10 achieving normal protective sensation by 12 months**.
- **Where it was done, and why that matters:** the cells are derived from **healthy placental tissue donated following childbirth** — tissue typically discarded after delivery. The trial was conducted in **Nicaragua**, approved by the **Ministry of Health of Nicaragua**, with investigators from the Wake Forest Institute for Regenerative Medicine and Wake Forest University School of Medicine, two Nicaraguan hospitals and the University of North Carolina.
- **Registry (v2 API; robots.txt read first this session):** **NCT06373809** is a **completed early Phase 1** dose-escalation safety and exploratory efficacy study of transplanted placenta-derived stem cells, **20 participants actual**, sponsor **Michael H Carstens**, collaborators the Ministerio de Salud de Nicaragua and the Wake Forest Institute for Regenerative Medicine, actual start October 8, 2021, **actual primary completion and completion March 31, 2024**, last update posted **April 18, 2024**, **two sites in Nicaragua**, intervention "Transplantation of Placenta Derived Stem Cells" administered subcutaneously into foot, ankle and calf tissues. **The item states both caveats:** the paper treats **10 patients** against a registry enrolment of **20**, so 20 is a target rather than a treated population; and the record has not been updated since **April 18, 2024**, more than two years before this cycle's cutoff.
- **Timing:** the paper was published **October 16, 2026** and the release reached the wire on **November 12**. The item is published on the strength of the peer-reviewed paper, not the embargo, and the ledger records both dates.

## Notes from this cycle

- **The registry can contradict a company on a denominator, and that is an item, not a nitpick.** MeiraGTx reports LUMEOS as n=95; NCT04671433 records **105 actual** participants and the follow-up NCT04794101 records 97. The published item carries both numbers and says plainly that the release does not explain the difference. A reader planning around a filing should not have to discover the discrepancy by hand.
- **An Item 1.01 stock purchase agreement is a better source for deal terms than the press release.** Lineage's release headlined "$4.7 million in cash and equity at closing and up to $5 million on the first anniversary"; the 8-K itself gives $2.4M cash plus 2.2 million shares valued at $2.1M, separate $0.2M expense-fund and $1.7M third-party payments, **up to $5.4 million** at the first anniversary and **up to $48.0 million** in milestones. The item uses the filed figures.
- **An acquirer can inherit a grant co-funding obligation along with the asset.** Lineage assumed up to **$3.1 million** of CIRM co-funding through February 2030, replacing CIRM's own arrangement. That clause is invisible in the press release and is the kind of term a CDMO reader needs.
- **A registry record can predate the transaction that makes it interesting.** REGEN4HD is still registered under **Leslie Thompson**, the seller's representative, because the record was last updated April 13, 2026. The item names the sponsor as the registry has it and explains why.
- **A CDMO partnership announcement is publishable only if the item says what does not exist yet.** The Andelyn/Celliaz item states that no trial is registered, no IND is cleared and no patient has been dosed, and that the release itself calls the Phase 1/2 *planned*.
- **Peer-reviewed beats embargo, and both dates belong in the ledger.** The placenta-derived stem cell paper was published October 16; the release surfaced November 12. The item rests on the paper.
- **The scope filter did real work tonight.** Biolojic's IND clearance and City Therapeutics' first dosing are both genuine, primary-verified clinical/regulatory events, and both were held because the modality is an ADC and an oligonucleotide respectively. Recording the reasons means the next cycle does not re-derive them.
