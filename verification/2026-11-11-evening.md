### 1. ImvivaBio reports longer-term CTA313 data in lupus, with DORIS remission in 88% at 12 months

- **Primary source:** [ImvivaBio release, November 11, 2026 (GlobeNewswire via The Manila Times)](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/11/11/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/imvivabio-presents-longer-term-data-from-clinical-studies-of-cta313-allogeneic-car-t-therapy-in-lupus-at-the-american-college-of-rheumatology-convergence-2026/2467530) — HTTP 200, body read directly.
- **Product:** CTA313, an investigational **dual-targeted CD19/BCMA allogeneic CAR-T cell therapy** for systemic lupus erythematosus and lupus nephritis.
- **Framing, kept precise:** the data were **shared in four poster presentations** at ACR Convergence 2026 (November 6–11, Orlando), with follow-up of up to 12 months. The release is a conference summary, not a peer-reviewed publication.
- **Pooled Phase 1 dataset (14 patients):** heavily pretreated refractory SLE with minimal renal involvement; median of five prior therapies; 86.7% on concomitant tacrolimus. Dose-dependent expansion across the 3, 6 and 10 × 10⁶ cells/kg dose levels. B cells fell from a median 52.2 to 0 cells/µL by Day 7 and reconstituted around Day 42 with a predominantly naive phenotype; anti-dsDNA antibodies undetectable by Month 3; C3 rose from 0.65 to 1.34 g/L by Day 28.
- **Efficacy, denominators as the release states them:** SRI-4 response 91%; DORIS remission **73% at Month 6 (n=11)**; DORIS remission **88% at Month 12 (7 of 8 patients)**; at Month 6, 92% of evaluable patients (n=12) on glucocorticoids ≤5 mg/day and 92.9% (13 of 14) off immunosuppressants at cutoff.
- **Immune-reset analysis:** reconstituted B cells >95% naive; single-cell analysis of two patients showed no overlap between pretreatment autoreactive clones and newly formed clones; anti-dsDNA undetectable up to one year; complement normalized. In that dataset 69% reached low-dose glucocorticoid use; immunosuppression-free remission in 61% of SLE/LN patients and 90% of non-renal SLE; DORIS remission exceeded 70% from Month 3 through Month 12 in non-renal SLE; **complete renal remission in 50% (7 of 14) of LN patients at Month 4**.
- **Safety:** CRS in **35.7%, all Grade 1**; **no neurotoxicity and no GvHD**; Grade ≥3 neutropenia 71.4%, thrombocytopenia 21.4%, infections 21.4%.
- **The cross-study comparison is labelled as one:** a third poster compared single-target CD19 (n=17) against dual-target CD19/BCMA (n=27) from **separate Phase 1 studies**, reporting deeper B-cell eradication and DORIS remission in 80% versus 20% at Month 12 in SLE with minimal renal involvement, with no Grade ≥3 CRS for dual-target and no ICANS with either, but more hypogammaglobulinemia (85% versus 12%). The item states that this is a cross-study comparison, not a randomized one.
- **Registry (v2 API; robots.txt read first this session):** NCT07783269 — **ALLNEW**, a single-arm open-label multi-center Phase 1b basket study in active autoimmune diseases (SLE, progressive MS, autoimmune encephalitis): **NOT_YET_RECRUITING**, 81 estimated, sponsor Imviva Bio, estimated start December 2026, estimated primary completion June 2029, estimated completion September 2029, last update posted 2026-08-24, **zero locations listed**. A sponsor sweep for Imviva returns only two records — this one and NCT07070219 (CTD402 in T-ALL/LBL, unrelated). **The 14-patient pooled dataset has no registry record with results posted.**
- **Two cautions carried in the published item:** the 88% Month 12 DORIS figure rests on **eight** patients, and the 14-patient cohort is heavily pretreated with low renal involvement, so these are not generalisable SLE or LN results.
- **A false claim in the prepared draft was caught and corrected before publication.** The draft's closing paragraph asserted "this bulletin reported CTA313 on November 2 in the context of progressive multiple sclerosis." **It did not.** The Nov. 2 morning and evening ledgers both list CTA313 under *screened/held*, not published — the only data then were an October 21 NeurologyLive report and a not-yet-recruiting registry record. A site-wide grep confirms `CTA313` and `ImvivaBio` appeared **zero** times on any of the five pages before this cycle. The published sentence now says this is the bulletin's **first published item on CTA313**, and explains that Nov. 2 was a hold.
- **A fourth defect, found only by going to the abstract itself: the release and the published abstract are different data cuts.** The ACR abstract for the pooled CTA313 lupus analysis is reachable and read directly — **Abstract Number: 0260**, (0253-0287) Systemic Lupus Erythematosus - Treatment Poster A, Poster Session A, **Sunday, November 8, 2026**, session time 10:30AM-12:30PM, *Arthritis Rheumatol.* 2026; 78 (suppl 9). Its data cut is **1 June 2026** and it reports the same 14 patients with **different numbers** from the company release: CRS in **28.6%** (all Grade 1) against the release's 35.7%; DORIS remission **80% at Month 12 (4 of 5)** against the release's 88% (7 of 8); Grade ≥3 neutropenia **64.3%** and lymphopenia 42.9% against the release's 71.4%; and **85%** reaching low-dose glucocorticoid use against the release's 69%. The release also says CTA313 is "powered by ANSWER technology," which the abstract states and the release does not. The published item now names which document each figure comes from, so a reader who checks the abstract does not read a contradiction into the bulletin. Five CTA313 abstracts are posted for the meeting — the pooled analysis, the immune-reset analysis, the lupus-nephritis analysis, the single-vs-dual-target comparison and a preliminary safety poster — which is why the release says "four poster presentations" and the site publishes the release's count.

### 2. Capricor's deramiocel reaches an FDA decision on November 22 in Duchenne

- **Primary source:** [Capricor release, November 10, 2026 (GlobeNewswire via The Manila Times)](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/11/11/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/capricor-therapeutics-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-provides-corporate-update/2466987) — HTTP 200, body read directly. Dateline **SAN DIEGO, Nov. 10, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE)**.
- **Status:** FDA is reviewing the **Biologics License Application for deramiocel**, an allogeneic cardiosphere-derived cell therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy, with a **PDUFA target action date of November 22, 2026**.
- **What extended the review:** in **August** the company submitted two-year open-label extension data plus additional robustness analyses from HOPE-3 and an integrated efficacy summary across HOPE-2 and HOPE-3. FDA classified it as a **major amendment, extending the review by three months** to November 22. The submission also proposed a refined indication focused on **upper limb impairment in patients older than nine years** — the population studied in HOPE-3 and the focus of its primary endpoint.
- **The pivotal data are peer-reviewed, and the effect size is now given:** HOPE-3 was published in *The Lancet* on **August 22, 2026** (408(10556):721-733, published online July 29, 2026, doi 10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01385-1, **PMID 42526472**). The PubMed abstract gives the primary endpoint as total PUL2.0 percentage change from baseline at 12 months, favouring deramiocel by **4.55 percentage points (95% CI 0.47–8.63; p=0.029)**, in 106 randomised patients (54 deramiocel, 52 placebo) randomised 1:1, double-blind, placebo-controlled, with participants aged 10 years or older and dosing every three months in outpatient settings. Safety profile similar to placebo.
- **The two-year extension data are company-reported and not randomized:** at the 31st World Muscle Society Congress in October, patients who switched from placebo to deramiocel after year one declined **1.56 points less on PUL 2.0** than in their year on placebo — a 76% slowing — which the release itself describes as an **open-label, cross-phase comparison**.
- **Cardiac data:** on November 7 Jonathan Soslow of Vanderbilt presented results showing slower progression of myocardial fibrosis with deramiocel versus placebo and, in patients with cardiomyopathy, LVEF favouring deramiocel, "generally consistent when compared with a matched natural history group." The HOPE-3 cardiac results received the AHA Outstanding Research Award in Pediatric Cardiology.
- **The inspection is not omitted:** FDA conducted a **Bioresearch Monitoring (BIMO) inspection in July 2026** and issued a **Form 483 with one observation**; the company says it has submitted its response and is awaiting FDA feedback.
- **Manufacturing and money:** the San Diego GMP facility is operational and "positioned to support an initial commercial launch, if Deramiocel is approved"; a second-floor cleanroom expansion is underway with validation targeted for the first half of 2027. Cash, cash equivalents, restricted cash and marketable securities **approximately $199 million** as of September 30, 2026. Regulatory engagement has begun in Europe and Japan; non-deramiocel programmes, including the exosome platform, have been slowed pending regulatory clarity.
- **Registry (v2 API):** NCT05126758 (HOPE-3) — active, not recruiting, Phase 3, randomized double-blind placebo-controlled, **106 participants (actual)**, sponsor Capricor Inc., actual start **June 22, 2022**, **actual primary completion June 18, 2025**, estimated completion March 2027, last update posted August 28, 2026, **20 US sites**. NCT04428476 (HOPE-2 OLE) — active, not recruiting, Phase 2, 13 participants, last update posted September 22, 2026, five US sites. NCT03406780 (HOPE-2) — completed, Phase 2, 20 participants, last update posted May 28, 2025, nine US sites.
- **Scope note:** deramiocel is **not** on FDA's approved cellular and gene therapy products list — it is under review, not approved — and every timeline in the item is the company's projection, not a validated schedule.
- **A second defect caught before publication.** The prepared industry headline read *"Capricor heads to an FDA decision on November 22 for deramiocel — **the first cell therapy in Duchenne to reach a PDUFA date**."* The company release makes **no such claim**, and the claim is not defensible. A registry sweep of Duchenne-condition interventional studies returns **24 records**; after removing five that are Capricor's own and two that matched on text only — **SAT-3247** (an oral AAK1 inhibitor, NCT07287189, NCT06565208) and **PGN-EDO51** (an exon-51 oligonucleotide, NCT06079736, NCT06833931) — the remaining Duchenne cell-therapy programmes are UC-MSC (NCT06579352), EN001/ENCell myoblast-derived cells (NCT05338099, NCT06328725), MyoPAXon iPSC-derived muscle progenitor cells (NCT06692426) and a set of older mesenchymal-stromal-cell and bone-marrow transplant studies. None is a cell-therapy BLA in the registry, and **the registry records no regulatory action date at all**, so the sweep cannot settle a "first to reach a PDUFA date" question in either direction. The headline now reads *"a Duchenne cell therapy with a decision date eleven days away"*, and the body carries the sweep with its predicate and its limit.
- **A third correction:** the draft body said HOPE-3 had an "estimated primary completion June 18, 2025." The registry field is **ACTUAL**, not estimated, with estimated completion March 2027. The published text says so.

## A defect found in this cycle's own publishing pipeline

**The auto-refresh cron job committed and pushed this cycle's content mid-run, including content that had not yet been verified.** The `refresh-bulletins.sh` cron job runs every 30 minutes and does `git add index.html industry.html executive.html visual.html roslinct.html` followed by a commit and a push. At 20:30 ET it picked up the working tree while the evening publisher had written its items but before the verification pass had finished, and pushed it to `origin/main` and to GitHub Pages — so the *unverified* text of the six morning items, the CTX310 enrichment, and the two evening items went live. The four defects found by this evening's verification pass (the unsupported "first cell therapy in Duchenne" headline, the false LUMINA naming caution, the false "this bulletin reported CTA313 on November 2" sentence, and the wrong ACR abstract number 2422875 — the abstract is 2216) were published and live for roughly 20 minutes before this pass corrected them. A fifth, the ImvivaBio release-versus-abstract data-cut difference, was found before this pass's final regeneration and never reached the live site.

What was done about it:

1. The refresh script was **neutralized** (backed up to `/private/tmp/refresh-bulletins.sh.orig`, replaced with an `exit 0` stub) for the duration of the corrected run. **It is restored after the verified push.** The script only `git add`s the five HTML files, so the stub can never itself be committed.
2. The macOS crontab could not be edited directly — `crontab` writes hang in this environment (a TCC authorization prompt that never surfaces). The cron entry is therefore still present and will resume running once the script is restored; the schedule itself was left untouched rather than being modified blind.
3. The pages were restored to the pre-publication baseline commit `958ad26` and the whole morning + evening sequence was regenerated in one pass from the corrected content, so no partially-verified text survives in the final commit.
4. The final tree was validated against a clean copy of the `958ad26` baseline (`/private/tmp/n11_baseline`) rather than against an intermediate backup, so the validator compared the true before/after.

**The generalisable lesson:** on this site the publisher and the cron job share the same working tree, so a manual publication run and a scheduled refresh can interleave. **A publication run must not begin while the refresh job can commit the tree, and a manual run must be re-verified against a known baseline commit before it is pushed** — because "the pages are already live" is not the same as "the pages are correct."

## Notes from this cycle

- **Read the primary release before writing a headline.** The Capricor "first cell therapy in Duchenne to reach a PDUFA date" framing came from the draft, not from the company. The release makes no such claim. A superlative that no source makes is a superlative this bulletin has invented.
- **A registry sweep can fail in a way that is only visible if you look at the interventions.** The Duchenne sweep returned 24 records, but SAT-3247 is an **oral AAK1 inhibitor** and PGN-EDO51 is an **exon-51 oligonucleotide** — both matched the "cell" text filter without being cell therapies. The predicate has to be checked against the intervention type, not the query string.
- **The registry does not record PDUFA dates.** That is the deeper reason the "first to reach a PDUFA date" question is unanswerable here, and it is now stated in the item rather than papered over.
- **A "first published item" claim must be checked against the ledgers, not against the drafts.** The claim that the bulletin had covered CTA313 on November 2 was false, and the Nov. 2 ledgers showed it plainly: CTA313 was in the *held* section of both cycles.
- **The LUMINA naming caution was right about a collision and wrong about everything else.** There is a registry collision — **NCT03711929, a terminated Phase III ophthalmology study of intravitreal sirolimus for posterior uveitis, sponsored by Santen** — but the bulletin had never used the name LUMINA for anything, and neither "luminance" nor "Luminary Therapeutics' LMY-922" is a retinal gene-therapy programme. The caution now names the actual collision.
- **A company name and a registry acronym can differ.** "ALLNEW" is ImvivaBio's real study name for NCT07783269, not a typo — it appears five times in the release.
- Every published link was HTTP-checked before commit (81 new or touched URLs, all 200 or 203); pages passed tag-balance, duplicate-id, placeholder-link and bare-ampersand checks against the pre-edit baseline; the per-note totals ownership assertion passed on all five pages.
