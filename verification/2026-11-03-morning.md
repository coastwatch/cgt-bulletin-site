### FDA finalizes CGT potency assurance guidance

- **Official notice:** Federal Register document 2026-22427, published Nov. 3, 2026, 91 FR 69259; FDA docket FDA-2023-D-4299. The notice states this final guidance replaces the same-title draft issued Dec. 28, 2023 (88 FR 89707).
- **Originals checked:** [Federal Register page](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/03/2026-22427/potency-assurance-for-cellular-and-gene-therapy-products-guidance-for-industry-availability), [Federal Register full text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/11/03/2026-22427.txt), and [GovInfo official PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-11-03/pdf/2026-22427.pdf). The notice and full text were read. The notice describes a five-part potency assurance approach: manufacturing process design, manufacturing process control, material control, in-process testing, and potency lot-release assays; it also says the guidance is nonbinding and discusses changes made from the 2023 draft.
- **Important boundary:** FDA's guidance landing page currently serves the 2023 draft and does not expose the final guidance PDF. The bulletin therefore states only technical detail expressly included in the final Federal Register notice and says the detailed recommendations should be read in the final guidance itself. The notice summary says assay recommendations are necessarily general because products and assays are product-specific.

### Wake Forest preclinical CLCN5 gene-therapy comparison in Dent disease type 1

- **Peer-reviewed source:** Lyu et al., “Renal CLCN5 gene therapy for Dent disease type 1: local rapamycin immune modulation and age-dependent outcomes with lentiviral and AAV9 vectors,” *Gene Therapy*, published online Nov. 2, 2026; PMID 42972019; DOI 10.1038/s41434-026-00655-9. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42972019/) and [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41434-026-00655-9) were checked; the abstract was retrieved through NCBI E-utilities.
- The abstract reports a ClC-5-null mouse model. Kidney lentiviral CLCN5 plus local rapamycin-loaded microspheres reduced urinary β2-microglobulin for up to 10 months. Neonatal AAV9 produced transient reductions despite persistent expression; adult AAV9 expression lasting 8–10 months did not consistently reduce urinary β2-microglobulin, even with rapamycin. The conclusion is that vector, immune modulation and developmental stage influence outcomes, and sustained expression alone is insufficient for durable phenotypic correction.
- **Disclosure:** PubMed's supplied abstract states Wake Forest Baptist Medical Center filed a patent related to this work. The bulletin calls this preclinical mouse research and makes no human safety/efficacy claim.
- **Registry handling:** ClinicalTrials.gov `robots.txt` was read before considering a registry query. It disallows `/api/`; no v2 API request was made, and no claim about absence of a registered human trial is published. The earlier draft's registry-absence claim and study link were removed.
