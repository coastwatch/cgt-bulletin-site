### 1. A single AAV injection tested as an alternative to lifelong daily leptin injections in congenital generalised lipodystrophy

- **Primary source:** Sommer, Roumane, Tiwari, Han, Heisler, Mcilroy and Rochford, "AAV-mediated delivery of leptin but not adiponectin improves metabolic health in a pre-clinical mouse model of congenital generalised lipodystrophy," *Gene Therapy* — doi 10.1038/s41434-026-00646-w, PMID 42919845. The full PubMed record was retrieved through eutils and the abstract read directly; the article was **online October 23, 2026** (received June 16, 2026; accepted September 21, 2026).
- **The companion release:** [University of Aberdeen, "Gene therapy advance for life-limiting body fat condition," November 24, 2026](https://www.abdn.ac.uk/news/26573/) — HTTP 200, body read directly.
- **The clinical context, from the paper:** recombinant leptin injected daily "is currently the only specific, approved treatment" for congenital generalised lipodystrophy (CGL), where severely reduced adipose tissue mass causes hepatic steatosis and lipoatrophic diabetes, and where both leptin and adiponectin are dramatically reduced.
- **The result:** AAV-mediated leptin delivery "significantly improved hepatic steatosis and hyperinsulinemia" in a well-characterised murine model. The university adds that a single injection produced dramatically reduced fatty liver, fewer diabetes markers and lower hunger "for a period of at least two months."
- **The negative result, published alongside the positive one:** "adiponectin delivery did not lead to any observed beneficial effects." Because the consequences of adiponectin loss in CGL "are not fully understood" and the therapeutic benefit of delivering it is unclear, the paper frames this as narrowing what a lipodystrophy gene therapy has to deliver. The item states this explicitly rather than reporting only the positive arm.
- **The manufacturing burden, from the release:** the injections are "especially painful to administer for people who lack subcutaneous fat, the layer of fat under the skin," must be kept refrigerated, and "limit the ability to work and travel." The paper's own conclusion is that gene therapy may offer long-term leptin delivery "without the need for continuous supply of perishable therapeutics and painful daily injections."
- **What is NOT claimed, and the item says so:** this is preclinical work in mice. The university states lower doses "are now being tested" and that "a different AAV would be needed for use in people." **No ClinicalTrials.gov record could be located:** a v2 sweep on leptin plus (gene therapy or AAV) plus lipodystrophy returned only two unrelated records (NCT01414660, a completed transplant-immunology study in Lille, and NCT06236932, an infectious-disease susceptibility study in obesity and lipodystrophy in Naples), neither of which is a trial of this therapy. The item therefore reports no clinical stage, no trial identifier and no registry anchor.
- **Duplication check:** grepped all five pages for "lipodystrophy", "leptin", "Rowett", "metreleptin", "Myalept", "MeSmoGen" and "congenital generalized lipodystrophy" — zero hits for the programme.
- **Funding and patient context, carried because they bear on how this will develop:** funded by the East of Scotland Bioscience Doctoral Training Partnership, the BBSRC and Diabetes UK (which has awarded new funding to take the work forward), and welcomed by Lipodystrophy UK and Lipodystrophy United.

### 2. Triple Helix Science reports Yamanaka reprogramming factors delivered into the human brain

- **Primary source:** [Triple Helix Science release via EIN Presswire, November 24, 2026, 11:00 GMT](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/958534081/triple-helix-science-reports-first-yamanaka-factor-gene-therapy-delivered-into-the-human-brain) — HTTP 200, body read directly. The company's own [site](https://www.triplehelixscience.com/) was fetched and confirms the release and its pipeline framing.
- **The claim:** five patients with stroke- or dementia-related neurological decline received an investigational intranasal gene therapy between February and April 2026. "To the company's knowledge, these are the first in-vivo administrations of Yamanaka reprogramming factors in humans, and the first to deliver them into the brain." All five gave informed consent and remain under clinical follow-up.
- **The construct:** eleven genes. Oct4, Sox2 and KLF7 are associated in the research literature with partial epigenetic reprogramming, intended to shift gene expression in aged or injured cells "without erasing cell identity"; the other eight (hTERT, GBA, BDNF, FGF21, FOXO3, PGC-1 alpha, Klotho, IL-10) were selected for neuroprotective, metabolic, protein-clearance and anti-inflammatory activity. The factors derive from Shinya Yamanaka's Nobel Prize-winning work.
- **Delivery and the safety architecture:** AAV vectors are deposited into the nasal submucosa after topical anesthesia; hyaluronidase is administered first to open the tissue plane and improve distribution; the vector travels along the olfactory and trigeminal pathways, "bypassing the bloodstream and the blood-brain barrier." Oct4, Sox2, KLF7 and hTERT sit under an inducible switch active only during defined medication-controlled windows, and the three reprogramming and growth-promoting genes carry a controllable safety gene allowing expression to be shut down with a specific drug. The company's stated reason: "Oct4 and Sox2 have recognized oncogenic potential," and the control mechanisms are in the design to address that risk, with ongoing monitoring.
- **Safety and efficacy, kept strictly apart:** across the five patients "there have been no complications and no treatment-related adverse effects." **No efficacy is claimed** — the chief science officer states that "no conclusions about effect can be drawn at this stage" and that "formal before-and-after assessment is required to measure the effect in any individual." The company also frames the field: "Translation to human neurological conditions is at an early stage across the field."
- **Two gaps the item reports rather than papers over:** (a) **no ClinicalTrials.gov record could be located** — v2 sweeps on "Triple Helix Science", "intranasal AAV neurological" and "OCT4 SOX2 KLF7 hTERT BDNF" all returned zero matching records, and the release names no trial identifier and no registry; (b) the release **states no IND number and no trial phase**. It describes the therapy as investigational and the company's pipeline as Phase 1, but the specific five-patient study is not tied to a public record.
- **Duplication check:** grepped all five pages for "Triple Helix", "Yamanaka", "OCT4", "SOX2" and "reprogramming" — zero hits.
