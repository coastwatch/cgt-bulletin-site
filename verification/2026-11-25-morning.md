### 1. FDA accepts AnGes's rolling BLA submission for its HGF gene therapy in limb ischaemia

- **Primary sources, both read directly:**
  - [AnGes Inc. notice, November 25, 2026, original Japanese](https://www.anges.co.jp/irinformations/qW6rr46nnimgksnIhGLl) — HTTP 200, body read directly.
  - [AnGes Inc. official English translation (PDF)](https://files.anges.co.jp/uploads/2026-11/6205a7c6-f78a-49e4-8b4e-ebc2f7ec42f8.pdf) — HTTP 200, 2 pages, text extracted locally. The PDF carries the note "This document has been translated from the Japanese original for reference purposes only. In the event of any discrepancy between the two, the original shall prevail." **Both documents were read and are consistent.**
- **The announcement:** FDA "has accepted the Company's request for a Rolling Submission of its Biologics License Application (BLA), as well as its proposed BLA submission plan, for the HGF gene therapy product currently under development for the treatment of Chronic Limb-Threatening Ischemia (CLTI/CLI) associated with Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD)." The product is under **FDA Breakthrough Therapy and Fast Track designations**. "The initial BLA submission package is planned to be submitted in December 2026." The release states the acceptance is not expected to affect consolidated financial results.
- **Why the modality is the story:** this is a **plasmid DNA** gene therapy — the HGF gene is encoded in plasmid DNA injected directly into the narrowed or occluded arterial segment to drive neovascularisation — so there is **no viral vector and no cell product** in the manufacturing sense. This is a materially different supply chain from the AAV and cell-therapy products this bulletin usually tracks.
- **The regulatory sequence, per the company's own release:** Aug. 8, 2025 — decision to complete US clinical development and proceed to BLA preparation, plus an **API supply agreement with Boehringer Ingelheim Biopharmaceuticals**; Jan. 6, 2026 — Type B clinical meeting; May 14, 2026 — Type B CMC meeting, with agreement on the regulatory strategy; July 17, 2026 — proceeding to BLA preparation **without a Pre-BLA meeting**. The item flags that the API supply question — the most common gene-therapy launch failure mode — was contracted two years before approval.
- **Clinical rationale, attributed:** PAD affects ~200 million people worldwide; the release cites Armstrong et al. that five-year mortality after major lower-extremity amputation is 57%, second only to lung cancer at 80%, and the Global Vascular Guidelines' recommendation to treat PAD early.
- **Japan context, from AnGes' own product page** (read this session): conditional and time-limited approval in **2019** for severe CLTI — described by the company as the world's first approval of a gene therapy using plasmid DNA — then a shift of US development toward milder-to-moderate CLTI after strong US Phase 2 results reported in June 2024, with FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation following in September 2024.
- **What is NOT verified in this cycle:** **no ClinicalTrials.gov record for the current HGF gene therapy trial could be located.** A v2 sweep on "HGF gene therapy" plus peripheral artery/PAD/critical limb ischaemia, and a sponsor sweep on AnGes, returned only the older **AMG0001** studies — **NCT04267640** (Phase 2 in PAD and CLTI, status UNKNOWN, last update Dec. 22, 2021) and **NCT02144610** (Phase 3 in critical limb ischaemia, status TERMINATED, last update Aug. 13, 2019) — plus unrelated COVID-19 and degenerative-disc studies. No trial identifier or IND number is reported, and the item says so.
- **Duplication check:** word-boundary greps across all five pages for AnGes, HGF, AMG0001, PAD, CLTI and Boehringer returned **zero** hits for the programme (the single "Boehringer" hit is about obrixtamig, unrelated).

### 2. multiQure licenses Apertura's TfR1 CapX blood-brain-barrier capsid for its Huntington's RNAi gene therapy

- **Primary source:** [multiQure S.A. release, November 25, 2026](https://multiqure.com/news/multiqure-announces-agreement-to-access-apertura-gene-therapys-tfr1-capx-a-next-generation-blood-brain-barrier-crossing-capsid/) — HTTP 200, body read directly. The company is a Warsaw Stock Exchange-listed developer of an RNAi gene therapy for Huntington's disease.
- **What was agreed:** multiQure will use Apertura's TfR1 CapX for its investigational RNAi programme. "The initial objective of multiQure's research with TfR1 CapX will be to determine in animal studies whether intravenous administration of multiQure's RNAi program for Huntington's disease achieves sufficient exposure in key brain structures." The company is also exploring the capsid for other pipeline programmes, quoting Sławomir Wójcik on an intravenous route being "minimally invasive and can potentially achieve broad distribution across the central nervous system." Apertura's Chief Business Officer Diego Garzón is quoted on the capsid's potential to "help unlock new approaches to treat Huntington's and other diseases of the central nervous system while making treatment less burdensome for patients."
- **The platform framing:** the mQ platform is being used in CAG repeat expansion disorders (polyglutamine diseases) such as Huntington's, where the mechanism is identical across conditions — allele-selective, RNAi-mediated silencing of the mutant allele.
- **Why the capsid matters more than the deal:** TfR1 CapX is not a single-use asset. Apertura licenses it repeatedly, and **this site already carries an agreement in the other direction — with Lexeo Therapeutics for a Friedreich's ataxia programme** ([BioSpace release](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/apertura-gene-therapy-to-provide-lexeo-therapeutics-access-to-tfr1-capx-for-program-designed-to-treat-friedreich-ataxia)). A capsid licensed to a second CNS programme, in a different modality, is a platform signal.
- **Manufacturing context, from the company's own November 19 release** (read this session): multiQure is moving MQ-201 into the manufacturing phase with **Andelyn Biosciences** (Columbus, Ohio), "a specialist gene therapy vector manufacturer whose facility has passed an FDA pre-license inspection," responsible for GMP manufacture of **both the plasmids and the AAV5 vector**. The company's framing of why this matters: "Viral vector manufacturing is one of the longest, most complex and most expensive parts of developing any gene therapy, and it largely determines when a therapy can reach patients." The first stage covers a vector-titer assay tailored to multiQure's expression cassette, plasmid preparation, and optimisation of cell culture and purification, culminating in a research-grade AAV5 batch in a **50 L bioreactor**, on the same suspension HEK293 platform that will produce GMP clinical material — so, in the company's words, "the process refined in the first stage will not need to be transferred to another platform, which reduces risk on the path to clinical manufacturing."
- **What is NOT verified:** **no ClinicalTrials.gov record could be located for MQ-201** — v2 sweeps on "MQ-201", on multiQure as sponsor, and on allele-selective RNAi in Huntington's disease all returned zero matching records — and no IND number is named in either release. The item reports the company's own "towards first-in-human dosing" phrasing and nothing beyond it.
- **Duplication check:** zero hits across all five pages for multiQure, Apertura, TfR1 or CapX.
