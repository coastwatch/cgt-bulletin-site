### 1. Second Complete Response Letter for deramiocel (Duchenne)

- **Primary source:** [Capricor Therapeutics — regulatory update on the deramiocel BLA, November 20, 2026](https://www.capricor.com/investors/news-events/press-releases/detail/360/capricor-therapeutics-provides-regulatory-update-on) — HTTP 200; release body read directly, dated Nov. 20, 2026, 7:55 p.m. ET. The release states the CRL says FDA "does not consider the data sufficient to establish substantial evidence of effectiveness and cites certain other clinical deficiencies"; the company did not describe those deficiencies. A conference call and webcast is scheduled for 8:30 a.m. ET on Monday, Nov. 23.
- **The arc, from the company's own release archive** (all fetched directly; the press-release listing was read to identify each release id):
  - **Jan. 20, 2026** — [release](https://www.capricor.com/investors/news-events/press-releases/detail/335/capricor-therapeutics-provides-regulatory-update-on): FDA had "formally requested the full HOPE-3 clinical study report (CSR) and supporting data to address the Complete Response Letter," and "did not request any additional clinical studies or new patient data." This release is the source for the July 2025 CRL date.
  - **Mar. 10, 2026** — [release](https://www.capricor.com/investors/news-events/press-releases/detail/338/capricor-therapeutics-announces-establishment-of-new-pdufa) and [Form 8-K, Items 7.01/8.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1133869/000110465926025657/capr-20260310x8k.htm) (HTTP 200 with an EDGAR user agent; 403 without one — a bot gate, not a dead link). The 8-K states FDA "has lifted the previously issued Complete Response Letter and resumed review," classifies the submission as a Class 2 resubmission with an Aug. 22, 2026 PDUFA target action date, and that "at this time, the FDA has not identified any potential review issues."
  - **Jul. 29/30, 2026** — [company release](https://www.capricor.com/investors/news-events/press-releases/detail/351/capricor-therapeutics-provides-update-on-fda-advisory) states the committee voted "3 for, 9 against, 0 abstain" that available evidence did not support effectiveness for DMD cardiomyopathy, that the voting question addressed a narrower indication than proposed, and that there was no benefit-risk vote. Cross-checked against [FDA's own meeting announcement](https://www.fda.gov/advisory-committees/advisory-committee-calendar/cellular-tissue-and-gene-therapies-advisory-committee-july-29-2026-meeting-announcement-updated) (HTTP 200), which confirms the date, **BLA 125842**, the product as "deramiocel (human allogeneic cardiosphere-derived cells)" and the indication as cardiomyopathy in DMD. The FDA page carries the agenda but not the vote tally, so the 3-9-0 figure is attributed to the company.
  - **Aug. 24, 2026** — [release](https://www.capricor.com/investors/news-events/press-releases/detail/354/capricor-therapeutics-announces-extension-of-pdufa-target): FDA accepted a major amendment adding 24-month open-label extension data and a refined upper-limb indication, extending the action date to Nov. 22, 2026.
- **Published HOPE-3 primary endpoint** is carried from the existing Nov. 10 item (Lancet 408(10556):721-733, doi 10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01385-1, PMID 42526472): PUL 2.0 least-squares mean percentage change 4.55 points favouring deramiocel (95% CI 0.47 to 8.63), p=0.029, ITT 106 (54 deramiocel, 52 placebo).

### 2. FDA establishes the CBER Safety Council

- **Sources:** [Pharmaceutical Technology, November 20, 2026](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/gene-therapy-developers-face-new-postmarket-fda-safety-council) — HTTP 200, full text read directly; and [Reuters, November 19, 2026](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/fda-sets-up-council-post-market-review-vaccines-gene-therapies-2026-11-19/), which returned HTTP 401 to plain fetches (Reuters 401s real and bogus URLs alike, so that status proves nothing about the URL). The council was established Nov. 19, 2026, sits within CBER reporting directly to the center director, replaces CBER's existing Safety Working Group, and covers vaccines, gene therapies and blood products; its mandate covers emerging safety concerns, labeling changes, risk-management measures, safety-related post-market studies and advice on possible market withdrawals, with coordination planned with CDC and NIH and reliance on VAERS and Sentinel. How that applies to ultra-rare cell and gene therapy populations is explicitly undefined.
- **Why it is not on FDA's committee pages — checked, not assumed:** the Safety Council is an **internal CBER body, not a Federal Advisory Committee**. FDA's advisory committee listings, its November 2026 press-announcement index and its 2026 biologics safety-and-availability index all carry no entry for it. All three were fetched and read this session. The item therefore says explicitly that a reader looking for it in the usual places will not find it, rather than implying the absence of coverage is an oversight.
- **Supporting primaries fetched:** [FDA CBER page](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-organization/center-biologics-evaluation-and-research-cber) (HTTP 200); [FDA's July 1, 2026 Casgevy supplemental approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapy-young-children-sickle-cell-disease) (HTTP 200, read directly — confirms first gene therapy approved for patients aged 2 and older with SCD, the CNPV pilot selection and the 53-day approval).
- **Label check on the Genglycos example (corrected before publishing):** a first draft of this item described the Genglycos label as carrying "the boxed warnings common to AAV gene therapies" and a "10-year disease monitoring program." Reading the [DailyMed SPL for GENGLYCOS](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/services/v2/spls/ef9144f5-10c3-47d9-a7c6-ab5ff11e9bf8.xml) (HTTP 200) showed **no boxed warning** — the relevant content is Warnings and Precautions 5.1 hypersensitivity and infusion reactions, 5.2 immune-mediated hepatotoxicity, 5.3 adrenal insufficiency including adrenal crisis, and 5.4 AAV vector integration and risk of tumorigenicity. The monitoring program is **two years** of safety and efficacy data from open-label commercial treatment of 50 patients and 20 controls, as a condition of accelerated approval, which the existing Aug. 19 item on this site already records. Both errors were corrected before the pages were written.
- **Scope:** a regulatory-process development affecting sponsors; not an approval, hold or safety action against any specific product.
