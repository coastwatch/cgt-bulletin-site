### 3. Systematic review separating conditioning toxicity from gene-editing risk in beta-haemoglobinopathies

- **Primary source:** Al-Subaie and Borgio, "Genotoxic safety and long-term engraftment dynamics of efficient translational CRISPR/Cas9 therapeutics for β-haemoglobinopathies from bench to clinical trials," *Gene Therapy*, published November 3, 2026; [doi 10.1038/s41434-026-00656-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41434-026-00656-8) — HTTP 200, article body read directly.
- **Scope and method:** PRISMA-based systematic review of **21 clinical and preclinical studies published 2014–2026**, inclusion criteria covering HbF induction, transfusion independence and safety outcomes.
- **Findings as reported:** exagamglogene autotemcel achieved transfusion independence in 91% of β-thalassemia patients and VOC-free status in 97% of sickle cell disease patients; severe adverse events including febrile neutropenia and stomatitis are primarily associated with **busulfan-based myeloablation rather than gene editing**; gene-editing safety and conditioning toxicity are interlinked because conditioning is part of the process and edited clones may interact with stress pathways during long-term repopulation; negligible off-target activity in most validated platforms, with rare complex structural rearrangements and intergenic off-target indels supporting the need for unbiased long-term genomic surveillance; the authors argue for a multilayer safety-risk screening and surveillance approach to pretransplant conditioning.
- **Framing:** labelled explicitly as a review aggregating 21 existing studies rather than new trial data, and noted as authored by two researchers in one field. The registry anchor linked is the pediatric exa-cel Phase 3 (NCT05329649), which the site already covers.
