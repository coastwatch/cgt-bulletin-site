### 2. Nature: first high-throughput in vivo CAR-T double-knockout CRISPR screen nominates NR4A1 with SOCS3

- **Primary source:** [Zhou L, Renauer PA, Tang K, Li S et al., "In vivo CAR-T double knockout screen maps synergistic antitumour gene pairs," *Nature*, published November 4, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10956-z) — HTTP 200, abstract and figure captions read. Subscription content beyond the abstract; no open-access full text was available, and the item is bounded to what the abstract states.
- **What the paper reports:** a high-throughput **in vivo CAR-T double-knockout (DKO) CRISPR screen** identifying DKO gene pairs that are both effective and synergistic. The top pair is **NR4A1 with SOCS3**. Whole-transcriptome profiling and in vivo scRNA-seq show the DKO specifically upregulates metabolic pathways in CAR-T cells, with significantly higher metabolic fitness and mitochondrial activity. NR4A1–SOCS3 DKO CAR-T cells and T cells showed exceptionally potent antitumour efficacy and long-term tumour control across different tumour models **without compromising safety features**, and outperformed several benchmark single-gene and dual-gene modifications in both T cells and CAR-T cells. The authors position the screen as a platform for rapid combination-target discovery and as establishing NR4A1 and SOCS3 as intracellular checkpoints.
- **Framing:** explicitly preclinical — the screen was run in vivo in mouse tumour models and the abstract reports no human data. A registry sweep for NR4A1 or SOCS3 in an intervention field returns no study, so nothing is in the clinic. The item contrasts this with the IL-2-signalling and exhaustion-resistance work the site has covered, noting that removing two inhibitory checkpoints is mechanistically distinct from restoring a missing cytokine signal, and that a double knockout changes the product specification rather than the manufacturing process.
- **Duplicate check:** no prior site coverage of NR4A1, SOCS3, double-knockout screening or the paper.
