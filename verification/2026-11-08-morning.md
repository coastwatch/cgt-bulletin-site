### Loop-structured CD38/CD123 bispecific CAR T-cells retain activity against antigen-loss AML, at an explicit cost in haematopoietic toxicity

- **Primary source:** Li W, Ma H, Yu H, Ding F, Huang Q, Wang S, Xu Y, Tang K, Xing H, Tian Z, Rao Q, Qiu S, Wang Y, Gu R, Wang M, Wang J, "Loop-structured CD38/CD123 bispecific CAR-T cells for antigen-heterogeneous acute myeloid leukemia," *Cancer Immunology, Immunotherapy*, published November 7, 2026 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00262-026-04623-5) — open access (CC BY-NC-ND). Crossref confirms the title, date and 16 authors; the Springer page and PDF routes both return a JavaScript challenge, so the article content was read via [Bioengineer's write-up of the study](https://www.bioengineer.org/loop-car-t-cells-target-heterogeneous-leukemia-antigens/) (November 8, 2026, HTTP 200), which quotes the paper's methods and results directly.
- **Design:** loop-structured bispecific CAR T-cells recognising **CD38 and CD123 simultaneously**, built to address antigen heterogeneity in AML. The team generated two reciprocal configurations — Loop 123 × 38 CAR and Loop 38 × 123 CAR — and compared them with monovalent CD38 CAR and CD123 CAR controls to isolate the effect of the loop architecture from the effect of dual recognition.
- **Results as reported:** both loop constructs recognised both antigens and showed significant activity against AML cell lines and primary samples expressing both. In a **12-day mixed-target co-culture, the monovalent products could not recognise the corresponding single-antigen-loss population, which accumulated, while both loop products suppressed outgrowth of both reciprocal antigen-loss populations.** Loop CAR T-cells reduced leukaemia burden and prolonged survival in cell-line-derived, patient-derived and mixed-antigen-loss xenograft models. The rationale was checked first by reanalyzing two public single-cell RNA-sequencing datasets (GSE241989 and SciLifeLab 23715648), which showed substantial interpatient heterogeneity with partially complementary transcript coverage.
- **The finding with the most clinical weight is the toxicity one, and it differs between the two constructs.** Because CD38 is expressed on normal haematopoietic progenitors, colony-forming assays were run on human umbilical cord blood CD34+ progenitors. Neither loop construct reduced colony formation at 24 hours, but at 72 hours the **Loop 123 × 38** group showed decreased BFU-E and CFU-GM formation while **Loop 38 × 123** did not, against vector-T controls — BFU-E and CFU-GM formation was significantly higher in the Loop 38 × 123 group (P < 0.01 and P < 0.0001 respectively). The authors conclude both configurations retain activity in antigen-heterogeneous models, with Loop 123 × 38 showing the stronger antileukaemic profile **but also the greater suppression of normal progenitor colonies**.
- **Not first in the clinic — bounded by a registry sweep.** ADAPT-HEM (NCT07523555) is a recruiting Phase 1/2 umbrella trial of biomarker-selected dual-target CAR T modules — CD19/CD22, CD19/CD20, BCMA/CD19 and BCMA/CD38 — in relapsed or refractory haematologic malignancies, 96 estimated, sponsor Beijing Biotech, actual start March 2, 2026, estimated primary completion March 14, 2027, last update posted April 13, 2026. Also in the registry: Shenzhen Geno-Immune's multi-target universal CAR T-cell study (NCT05995041, not yet recruiting, 30 estimated, CLL-1, CD33, CD38 and/or CD123-specific) and CART123 (NCT07464951, recruiting, 30 estimated, CD123-directed). So the honest framing is a new *design* — the loop architecture, and the explicit efficacy-versus-haematopoietic-toxicity trade-off between two configurations of it — not a new concept in dual-targeting.
- **Scope:** preclinical — cell-line and patient-derived assays plus xenograft models; no human administration and no registered study for this programme. Distinct from the already-covered Legend LB2501 item, which is an *in vivo* CD19/CD20 dual-targeting programme.

## Notes from this cycle

- **Two constructs in one paper can have opposite toxicity profiles, and that is the story.** The loop CAR T work is publishable precisely because the efficacy-versus-haematopoietic-toxicity trade between Loop 123 × 38 and Loop 38 × 123 is visible in the colony-forming data rather than buried in a discussion section.
- **A registry sweep is what stops a "new design" claim becoming a "first" claim.** The paper introduces loop-structured CD38/CD123 CAR T-cells; three registered dual-target or CD123-directed programmes mean the item says "a new design" and names the comparators, rather than implying an empty field.
- **Scope enforcement again beat newsworthiness.** A first-in-human result presented at the AHA with 31 patients and a clean safety profile was still held, because an siRNA is a drug. This is the second cycle the ARO-DIMER-PA data has been set aside on the same grounds.
