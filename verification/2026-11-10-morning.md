### 1. Beam Therapeutics: base-edited risto-cel met its primary endpoint in the pivotal BEACON trial

- **Primary source:** [Beam Therapeutics release, November 10, 2026 (GlobeNewswire)](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/11/10/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/beam-therapeutics-announces-positive-topline-pivotal-data-from-beacon-phase-12-trial-of-risto-cel-in-patients-with-sickle-cell-disease/2466257) — HTTP 200, body read directly (Beam's own IR site and GlobeNewswire's search/RSS both blocked; the Manila Times syndication mirror is the working route and is labelled as the company release).
- **Regulatory result:** BEACON achieved the primary efficacy endpoint of **freedom from severe vaso-occlusive crises for at least 12 consecutive months (VF12)** on independently adjudicated results, and **met all primary safety endpoints at the prespecified interim analysis**. All secondary efficacy endpoints were met, including freedom from inpatient hospitalisation for severe VOCs for 12 consecutive months. As of an **October 20, 2026** data cutoff, **39 adults and 10 adolescents had been treated** and **31 patients had completed the follow-up required for the VF12 endpoint**.
- **Safety:** findings consistent with previously reported BEACON data and with the known and intended effects of plerixafor use and the cell collection process, myeloablative conditioning with busulfan, the autologous HSCT process, and underlying SCD.
- **Next steps:** Beam expects to submit a US biologics license application **as early as year-end 2026**, which the company says would have the potential to be the first BLA for a base editing therapy; detailed pivotal results are planned for a medical meeting in 2027, with an earlier data cut-off to be presented orally at ASH on December 12–15, 2026.
- **Mechanism:** risto-cel is a one-time autologous cell therapy of CD34+ HSPCs base-edited in the promoter regions of the HBG1/2 genes, administered via a HSCT procedure. The edit is designed to stop BCL11A binding the promoter without disrupting BCL11A expression, raising production of non-sickling and anti-sickling fetal hemoglobin — the same therapeutic logic as Casgevy, but by base editing rather than nuclease disruption of BCL11A.
- **Registry (v2 API; robots.txt read first this session):** NCT05456880 (BEACON) — active not recruiting Phase 1/2 of a single dose of autologous CD34+ base-edited HSPCs in SCD and severe VOCs, 15 participants, sponsor Beam Therapeutics, actual start 2022-08-30, estimated primary completion 2028-02, last update posted 2025-12-16, 19 US sites. Long-term follow-up NCT07373639 — recruiting, 50 estimated, actual start 2025-12-15, estimated primary completion 2043-02-12, last update posted 2026-01-28, 17 US sites.
- **Discrepancy recorded rather than smoothed over:** the release describes 49 patients treated and 31 completing VF12 follow-up, while the registry record still shows 15 participants and was last updated in December 2025 — the registry has not caught up with the trial.
- **Boundaries carried:** an interim analysis of an open-label, single-arm trial, with the full dataset due in 2027. Beam itself lists as a risk factor whether topline results from a prespecified interim analysis will be consistent with final or more detailed results, and whether the results will suffice to support a BLA. **The VF12 figure is on the 31 patients with complete follow-up, not all 49 treated.**

### 2. EBMT publishes the first harmonised cryopreservation recommendations for academic CAR T-cell manufacturing

- **Primary source:** Bonig H, Calmels B, Giverne C, Köhl U, Kuball J, Lechanteur C, et al. (12 authors), "Cryopreservation of academic CAR T-cell products: methodological recommendations from the EBMT practice harmonisation and guidelines committee," *Bone Marrow Transplantation*, published November 9, 2026 (doi 10.1038/s41409-026-03020-x) — Crossref-confirmed title, date, journal and authorship; the [Nature article page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41409-026-03020-x) returned HTTP 200 and the abstract was read directly. Subscription content beyond the abstract; the item is bounded to the abstract and figure information.
- **The problem it addresses:** academic CAR T-cell manufacturing is expanding rapidly across Europe while cryopreservation practice remains heterogeneous, raising concerns about product comparability. **An EBMT survey of 52 academic centres** found wide variability in freezing timing, cryomedium composition, device choice, storage temperature and post-thaw quality control, and no harmonised guidance existed for CAR T-cell products.
- **The recommendations, as issued:** perform cryopreservation as soon as possible after manufacturing, **preferably within 48 hours**; begin freezing **within 30 minutes of cryomedium addition**; use controlled-rate freezing to at least **−80 °C**, with long-term storage **below −120 °C** wherever feasible; and treat **retention samples, post-thaw viability, identity testing and validated transportation procedures as mandatory**. Liquid nitrogen and Peltier-based controlled-rate freezing systems were regarded as technically equivalent, and **−80 °C passive freezing** — the workhorse method in most academic centres — was noted as successfully used and acceptable as a back-up method where local confirmation of suitability is provided through formal method validation.
- **Why it belongs in a manufacturing-facing bulletin:** this is the first harmonised reference for how an academic or hospital cell-therapy unit should freeze and store the product it manufactures itself. For centres without a licence agreement and a validated commercial freeze protocol, it supplies the specification — timing, temperature, device equivalence and the mandatory post-thaw and transport checks. It also makes the comparability problem explicit: if freezing practice varies across sites, differences in product characteristics cannot be attributed to the product alone.
- **Scope:** consensus-based technical guidance for groups establishing academic CAR T-cell manufacturing, drafted by an expert panel after an EBMT workshop using the society's harmonisation methodology, drawing on survey data, literature and professional experience. The site had no prior coverage of EBMT guidance or CAR T-cell cryopreservation.
