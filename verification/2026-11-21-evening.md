## Published

### 1. FDA's own briefing document for BLA 125842 — the pre-specified primary endpoint was not met

- **Primary sources, both FDA's own and both read directly:**
  - [FDA Briefing Document, BLA 125842/0 (deramiocel), for the Cellular, Tissue, and Gene Therapies Advisory Committee meeting of July 29, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/media/193839/download) — HTTP 200, PDF, 53 pages, text extracted locally and quoted.
  - [FDA Clinical Overview, BLA 125842 (deramiocel), July 29, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/media/193912/download) — HTTP 200, PDF, 131 pages, text extracted locally.
- **Why this is a new item and not a rehash of the morning cycle:** this site has covered the Capricor programme three times (Nov. 10 PDUFA item, Nov. 11 filing item, Nov. 21 morning CRL item) and every one of those was built on the company's release, the peer-reviewed *Lancet* paper, or the registry. FDA's own review file had never been read. It contains the agency's conclusions in its own words, and they differ from the company's.
- **FDA's quoted conclusions, taken verbatim from the briefing document:** "The results demonstrate that this pre-specified primary efficacy endpoint was not met." / "After extensive analyses of the submitted data both in the original BLA and in the BLA resubmission, the FDA review team was unable to identify a subpopulation that may potentially derive benefit from deramiocel." / "Considering the observed safety risks, which include hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis, the benefit-risk assessment for deramiocel appears unfavorable in the absence of evidence of effectiveness."
- **Primary endpoint across five analysis-plan versions (FDA's Table 7):** SAP 1.1 (prespecified MMRM, FDA analysis) 0.66 points, p=0.24 and 0.92 points, p=0.11 with imputation; SAP 1.7 0.61 points, p=0.64; SAP 2.0 2.38%, p=0.26; SAP 3.0 2.53%, p=0.21 and 4.16%, p=0.045; modified SAP 3.0 (the clinical study report) 4.55%, p=0.029. The last of these is the figure the *Lancet* published and the figure this site reported on Nov. 10 and Nov. 21.
- **Key secondary endpoint (LVEF at month 12), prespecified SAP 1.1 analysis:** between-group LS mean difference -0.041 percentage points (95% CI -2.58 to 2.49; p=0.97). The company's 91% slowing figure comes from a ranked-change ANCOVA that FDA says excludes 23 randomized subjects (22% of the randomized population), "violates the intention to treat principle," does not adjust for randomization stratification factors, and uses a ranked change that "has no clinical interpretation." FDA records that one excluded placebo subject had shown a 9.93 percentage-point LVEF increase at month 6 and three of the five excluded treated subjects had large LVEF decreases at month 6.
- **Analysis-plan timeline (FDA's Table 6):** randomized double-blind period completed June 18, 2025; SAP 2.0 and Protocol 9.0 created Sept. 26, 2025, submitted to FDA with "no FDA concurrence obtained" and some earlier FDA comments not incorporated; SAP 3.0 created Nov. 24, 2025 and "not submitted to FDA for review or comment"; database lock and unblinding Nov. 25, 2025. FDA: "SAP 3.0 which the Applicant considers as the final SAP was created/finalized one day prior to database lock/data unblinding of Study HOPE-3." The approved blinding plan stated the blinded biostatistics team at Pentara Corporation would develop the SAP; FDA records that "SAP versions 2.0 and 3.0 were created directly by the Applicant" and that audit-trail review "revealed the Applicant deviated from the pre-specified, approved blinding plan and the statistical programming and biostatistics functions were transferred from the outside, independent vendor to an in-house biostatistical team."
- **Functional unblinding:** hypersensitivity reactions 41.5% (22 of 53) deramiocel versus 15.4% (8 of 52) placebo, which FDA says "raises the possibility that treatment assignment could be inferred even under formal blinding conditions," with the open-label period extending the opportunity.
- **Safety (FDA's section 3.3):** 105 of 106 randomized subjects safety-evaluable; 53 deramiocel, 52 placebo; 6 serious adverse events in the randomized period (5 placebo, 1 deramiocel), including a Grade 4 life-threatening anaphylactic reaction in a placebo recipient that FDA attributes to product excipients, since the placebo contained all excipients except the cardiosphere cells.
- **Relationship to this site's earlier items:** the Nov. 10 and Nov. 21 morning items reported the 4.55-point, p=0.029 result as "the HOPE-3 primary endpoint," because that is what the peer-reviewed publication and the sponsor described. FDA's file shows that figure comes from the last of five analysis plans and that the prespecified analysis of the same trial was not significant. The evening item states this explicitly rather than leaving the two documents to be reconciled by the reader.

### 2. FDA's separate statement alleging "serious data integrity violations"

- **Sources:** [BioPharma Dive, "FDA again turns back Capricor Duchenne cell therapy," Nov. 21, 2026](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/capricor-fda-duchenne-cell-therapy-deramiocel/875384/) — HTTP 200, full text read directly; and [Tech Times, Nov. 21, 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/331238/20261121/fda-rejects-duchenne-cell-therapy-deramiocel-again-says-data-integrity-violations-cannot-fixed.htm) — HTTP 200, full text read directly. Both quote FDA's separate statement and its "serious data integrity violations" language; both attribute it to FDA rather than to the company.
- **The three named findings as reported:** unauthorized post hoc changes to the statistical analysis plan; violations of study blinding procedures; additional protocol violations — which FDA says "individually and together, make the data unreliable and uninterpretable as substantial evidence of effectiveness."
- **Two facts carried precisely because they cut against the company's interest, not for it:** (a) a **Regulatory Responsibility Letter** dated the same day addresses the inspection observations and Capricor can respond to it; FDA states a Form 483 is not a final agency determination and does not list every problem; (b) FDA's **July briefing document did not mention an inspection at all** — it raised analysis-change and possible-unblinding concerns from the data and recorded that Bioresearch Monitoring inspection assignments had been issued for the sponsor and one clinical site with inspections then ongoing. The inspection finding is new in this decision.
- **Corroborated by the patient-advocacy primary:** the [Muscular Dystrophy Association statement of November 21, 2026](https://www.mda.org/press-releases/statement-from-the-mda-on-the-fda-decision-not-to-approve-deramiocel-for-duchenne-muscular-dystrophy) was read directly (HTTP 200). It records the CRL as "identifying concerns about the reliability of clinical trial data" and states that "in a separate public statement, the FDA identified serious deficiencies in the conduct of the Phase 3 HOPE-3 study that, in its assessment, compromised the reliability of the clinical trial data." MDA "seeks to learn more and will report back to the community."
- **FDA's rare-disease framing, quoted:** "The FDA approaches rare disease applications with every intention of finding a path forward. However, flexibility in regulatory approach cannot come at the cost of the foundational requirement that the data underlying an approval decision be reliable."
- **Advocate counter-position:** more than two dozen trial investigators and physicians wrote to FDA before the decision, arguing rejection "would signal complete inflexibility on the part of the agency, contradicting the core values articulated" by its stance on rare disease reviews.
- **Where the programme stands:** Capricor is reviewing the letter, holds a conference call Monday, November 23, and says it has begun regulatory engagement in Europe and Japan. Deramiocel is not on FDA's approved cellular and gene therapy products list; no approval was granted, so nothing on the approved-products list changes.
