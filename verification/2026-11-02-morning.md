### 1. FDA draft guidance on individualized human genome editing products — the item held for four cycles

- **Primary source:** [Federal Register notice 2026-21954](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/28/2026-21954/individualized-human-genome-editing-products-considerations-for-bla-submission-and-postapproval), published October 28, 2026 — HTTP 200. The Federal Register **full-text API** (`/documents/full_text/text/2026/10/28/2026-21954.txt`) returns FDA's own notice text (HTTP 200), and the **draft guidance PDF** at `https://www.fda.gov/media/195887/download` returns HTTP 200 (393 KB, valid PDF). The notice text was read directly and quoted.
- Docket **FDA-2026-D-11783**; comments close **November 27, 2026**; submitting a comment requires the company's name/contact to be published in the public docket.
- Scope per FDA: recommendations for how an individualized genome editing product that includes multiple product variants can be submitted in a **single BLA under section 351(a) of the PHS Act** — how to specify the extent of genome editing component variation in a BLA, how to add new product variants after licensure, and related topics including potency and safety evaluation of variants, manufacturing process validation, labeling and indication statements, and drug naming and identification. It complements existing IND-stage CMC, nonclinical and clinical design, evidentiary-standards and individualized-therapy guidances.
- **Mechanism confirmed from primary sources:** the **individualized feature space (IFS)** — a sponsor defines the parameters of permitted variation in the original BLA, so a later variant can be added on laboratory evidence rather than a new clinical trial.
- **On-record FDA quotes** (BioSpace, November 2, 2026, [article](https://www.biospace.com/fda/plug-and-play-gene-therapy-fda-executes-on-vision-thats-been-in-the-works-for-years) — HTTP 200): Andrew Byrnes, director of the Division of Gene Therapy 1 at CBER, in an FDA webinar — "Think of the human genome as a massive instruction manual with three billion letters… A genetic disease can be caused by a single typo in these instructions, and different patients with that disease may have different typos." CBER director Karim Mikhail to BioSpace: the adapted pathway is designed to develop personalized gene therapies more quickly, and "the pace of the innovation in the science is incredible — in gene therapy, you have eight to 10 mutations being discovered a week."
- **Attribution kept separate:** H.C. Wainwright called the guidance a conversion of the individualized "product variant" concept into an operational licensing framework "with base editing the template modality"; BMO said Prime Medicine's platform approach "is poised to benefit." These are **analyst views on which programs could use the framework**; the guidance names no sponsor. Labelled as such in the item.
- **Boundaries stated:** draft guidance, non-binding, not for implementation; it complements rather than replaces the November 2025 Plausible Mechanism Pathway; it is separate from the Rare Disease Framework Initiative, which CBER director Michael Davis said is being stood up "pretty aggressively on a rapid timeline."
- **CMS claim deliberately not made:** CMS's CGT Access Model page, read directly, still describes the model's initial focus as sickle cell disease and does not itself document a broader scope. No expansion claim is published.
- **Note on the FDA guidance landing page:** the guidance document PDF and the Federal Register notice are both fetchable; the FDA guidance *landing page* URL could not be resolved (404 on every slug tried, and `fda.gov/search` returns an empty shell), so the PDF and the Federal Register notice are the linked originals rather than a guessed landing-page URL.

### 2. FDA accepts uniQure's BLA for AMT-130 in Huntington's disease — Priority Review, PDUFA May 1, 2027

- **Primary source:** [uniQure press release, November 2, 2026, filed as Exhibit 99.1 to Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1590560/000110465926120915/tm2628699d1_ex99-1.htm) and the [8-K itself](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1590560/000110465926120915/tm2628699d1_8k.htm) (Items 7.01, 8.01, 9.01) — both fetched and read. uniQure's own IR site returns 502, so the SEC-filed release is the linked primary, matching the pattern already used on this site for Iovance.
- BLA accepted for review under the **accelerated approval** pathway; **Priority Review** granted; **PDUFA target action date May 1, 2027**. In its acceptance letter FDA stated it is **tentatively planning to discuss the application at an Advisory Committee meeting** (date and timelines not yet set).
- **Supporting basis is an external control, not a concurrent comparator:** previously announced 36-month analysis from the Phase 1/2 studies against a propensity score-matched external control derived from the Enroll-HD natural history database.
- **Confirmatory study:** company and FDA have aligned on key design elements — randomized, standard of care, approximately **200 patients in a 2:1 ratio** of AMT-130 to standard of care, **Total Functional Capacity as primary endpoint at 36 months**, with US screening planned to start by the end of 2026. **No confirmatory trial is registered**: a sweep of the eleven uniQure-sponsored records returns only NCT04120493 (US, 43 participants, active not recruiting, start 2019-09-06, estimated primary completion 2029-06, last updated 2025-10-21) and NCT05243017 (Europe, 14 participants, active not recruiting, start 2021-10-07, estimated primary completion 2029-03, last updated 2025-03-10), and no 200-patient record. Phase 1/2 has dosed 51 patients in total.
- **Product:** miQURE platform, a microRNA designed to silence the huntingtin gene and the potentially toxic exon 1 protein fragment, delivered by a single administration through MRI-guided, convection-enhanced stereotactic neurosurgery directly into the striatum. If approved the company describes it as the first disease-modifying treatment for Huntington's disease; it holds Breakthrough Therapy, RMAT and Fast Track designations.
- **Weighed against prior coverage:** this is the same molecule and the same external-control dataset covered on September 29, when the four-year data undercut the three-year headline (44% slowing on composite cUHDRS at 48 months, non-significant at p=0.144). The acceptance is a regulatory milestone with a date attached, **not new efficacy data**, and a planned advisory committee indicates the agency has not settled on the evidentiary package. Stated explicitly in the item.
