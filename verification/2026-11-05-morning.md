### 1. Kamau Therapeutics: first multi-patient data for nula-cel, an autologous therapy that corrects the sickle mutation by homology-directed repair

- **Primary source:** Kamau Therapeutics press release, November 4, 2026 (GlobeNewswire) — read in full at the GlobeNewswire-syndicated mirror https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/11/04/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/kamau-therapeutics-announces-first-multi-patient-clinical-data-showing-precise-correction-of-the-sickle-cell-mutation-and-restoration-of-adult-hemoglobin/2461876 (HTTP 200). Kamau's own site returns a "Coming Soon" placeholder (its domain serves a GoDaddy placeholder, not the company site) and PR Newswire search returns no matching slug, so the syndication mirror is the readable original text and is labelled as the company release.
- **Status:** data accepted for an oral presentation at the 68th ASH annual meeting (December 14, 2026) — conference-abstract data, not a peer-reviewed paper. Three V2.0-treated patients (two with >6 months' follow-up) plus continued follow-up of the first nula-cel patient (V1.0 process) four years post-infusion.
- **Results as reported:** all three V2.0 patients achieved transfusion independence before hospital discharge; no vaso-occlusive events in any patient; neutrophil engraftment median 21 days (range 19–32); in the two patients with >6 months, total Hb >10 g/dL (10.2 and 11.1), HbS 17.6% at 12 months and 19.1% at 8 months, HbA 76.8% at 12 months and 66.8% at 8 months; bone marrow HDR allele frequencies stabilised at ~20%; single-cell analysis showed >90% of circulating RBCs with protective anti-sickling hemoglobin; no nula-cel- or procedure-related SAEs; AEs consistent with myeloablative busulfan conditioning and autologous transplant; off-target editing low. Manufacturing: drug product HDR allele frequency 32%–38% against a 20% target; median one apheresis cycle (range 1–3).
- **Registry (v2 API; robots.txt read first this session):** NCT04819841 — recruiting Phase 1/2 of nula-cel in autologous CD34+ cells converting HbS to HbA, 15 estimated, sponsor Kamau Therapeutics, actual start 2021-11-15, estimated primary completion 2026-12-31, last update posted 2026-07-02, six sites; no posted results.
- **Bounding the "first":** a registry sweep for homology-directed-repair / HBB gene-correction trials in sickle cell disease returned no other registered trial, so this is a new modality in this disease rather than a new target. It is also a different mechanism from Casgevy and other approved therapies, which knock down BCL11A to induce fetal hemoglobin rather than repairing HBB.
- **Caveats carried in the item:** three patients under one manufacturing version, two with >6 months' follow-up, single-arm, conference abstract, company statement that longer follow-up is essential; Fast Track and Orphan Drug designations held.

- **BIRD-1** is an adaptive, open-label Phase 1/2 of a single subretinal injection of OPGx-BEST1 in adults with Best vitelliform macular dystrophy (BVMD) or autosomal recessive bestrophinopathy (ARB). Cohort 2 dosed **4.5 × 10⁹ vg/eye, a three-fold increase** over Cohort 1, and enrolled **11 participants against an original target of five**. Topline three-month data expected Q1 2027; pivotal Phase 3 dosing anticipated H2 2027.
- **Cohort 1 results the release relies on** (reported September 2026, five participants at 1.5 × 10⁹ vg/eye): no SAEs, no DLTs, no intraocular inflammation, all treatment-related AEs mild or moderate; all five improved in at least one functional measure — BCVA 60% (3/5), LLVA 40% (2/5), contrast sensitivity 40% (2/5); 75% (3/4) of evaluable participants improved on microperimetry; four of five showed structural improvement. Company-reported, five patients, single-arm.
- **Registry:** NCT07185256 — recruiting Phase 1b/2a open-label dose-exploration basket study of subretinally injected OPGx-BEST1, 10 estimated, sponsor Opus Genetics, actual start 2025-09-25, estimated primary completion 2030-08, last update posted 2026-09-24, five US sites; primary outcomes are number of DLT events at the dose tested and number/severity of procedure-related AEs.

### 3. uniQure: final EPISOD1 results for AMT-162 in SOD1-ALS, from a discontinued programme

- **Primary source:** [NeurologyLive, November 4, 2026](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/final-results-reported-episod1-trial-sod1-als-gene-therapy-amt-162) (HTTP 200), covering the 2026 NEALS annual meeting (October 27–30, Clearwater, Florida), where Peter M. Andersen (Umeå University) presented with coinvestigators from Massachusetts General, Columbia and the University of Pennsylvania.
- **EPISOD1 (NCT06100276):** first-in-human, multicenter, open-label, single ascending-dose Phase 1/2 with planned five-year safety follow-up; **five participants dosed** (three low-dose, two mid-dose). One Grade 3 sensory-predominant neuropathy SAE determined to be a DLT in the mid-dose cohort; multiple Grade 2 sensory AEs in one low-dose participant. **CSF neurofilament light chain below baseline in 3 of 5** and **CSF SOD1 protein below baseline in 4 of 5** at six to twelve months. Investigators' conclusion: possible early biomarker changes including NfL reductions, but the serious and other adverse events produced an unfavourable benefit-risk ratio, leading to discontinuation. Long-term safety follow-up continues.
- **Product context:** AMT-162 is a one-time gene therapy using an AAVrh10 vector delivering an engineered microRNA to knock down SOD1, given intrathecally; it held FDA Orphan Drug and Fast Track designations. SOD1 mutations are thought to cause ALS via toxic gain of function and account for roughly 2% of ALS cases (fewer than 500 patients in the US).
- **Registry:** NCT06100276 — active not recruiting Phase 1/2, 20 estimated, sponsor uniQure Biopharma, actual start 2024-08-01, estimated primary completion 2026-09-30, last update posted 2025-10-22.
- **Timing gap recorded:** uniQure disclosed the discontinuation with its Q1 2026 results and, per NeurologyLive, did not detail the timing of the decision relative to the DLT. Comparison context included: tofersen (Qalsody), an ASO requiring periodic intrathecal injection, received accelerated approval in 2023 on plasma NfL reductions; Avrion's AAV9 candidate with EPFL is preclinical with first-in-human expected 2028.
