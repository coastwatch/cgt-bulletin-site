### 1. FDA is investigating a serious risk of malignancy linked to Zolgensma — the item this bulletin held for four cycles

- **Primary source:** [FDA, "FDA Investigating Serious Risk of Malignancy Linked to Zolgensma Gene Therapy," November 6, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/safety-availability-biologics/fda-investigating-serious-risk-malignancy-linked-zolgensma-gene-therapy) — HTTP 200, full notice read directly. Linked from FDA's own [Zolgensma product page](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/zolgensma), which lists it under "Latest Safety Information."
- **What FDA states:** it has received postmarketing adverse event reports of malignancies in patients treated with Zolgensma, an AAV9-based gene therapy for SMA, **"including confirmed reports of product-related insertional oncogenesis involving the GLI1 (Glioma-Associated Oncogene Homolog 1) gene."** GLI1 is an oncogene with the potential to cause cancer when overexpressed or abnormally activated. Reported tumour formations include tumours of the central and peripheral nervous systems, spinal/paraspinal regions, and kidney.
- **The mechanistic detail that makes this different from a signal:** **three cases involved tumours associated with GLI1**; in two of them, molecular analysis of tumour tissue **demonstrated Zolgensma AAV vector DNA integration into the host genome, with the promoter region of the onasemnogene abeparvovec construct inserted upstream of or within the GLI1 oncogene.** The mechanism of GLI1 overexpression in the third case remains under investigation.
- **Scope is wider than one product:** FDA has determined the risk of neoplasia, including malignancy, **applies to both onasemnogene abeparvovec products** — Zolgensma (intravenous, patients under two years of age) and Itvisma (intrathecal, patients two years and older) — because they contain the same vector and construct. There have been **no reported cases of neoplasia following Itvisma administration to date**. FDA is closely monitoring other similar AAV programmes.
- **Regulatory position:** FDA states the **overall benefits of Zolgensma and Itvisma continue to outweigh their potential risks for their approved uses**, that Novartis as sponsor is conducting long-term follow-up safety studies, and that FDA is expeditiously evaluating the adequacy of the current prescribing information and of long-term follow-up studies, **and is evaluating whether regulatory action is appropriate.** The current US prescribing information carries only a "theoretical risk of tumorigenicity due to integration of AAV vector DNA into the genome" under Warnings and Precautions. FDA directs that patients and clinical trial participants receiving these products **should be monitored for tumour formation**, and that providers who develop a tumour in a Zolgensma patient, or who wish to report other suspected adverse reactions, should contact Novartis Gene Therapies (1-833-828-3947) or FDA MedWatch. Initial approval of Zolgensma was May 24, 2019; Itvisma was approved November 24, 2025.
- **The two peer-reviewed precedents FDA itself cites**, carried in the item: a **GLI1** integration event in the FBX-101 programme (Vo JN et al., *N Engl J Med* 2026;395(15):1555–1558, doi 10.1056/NEJMc2607927, PMID 42874111) and a **PLAG1** integration event in the RGX-111 programme (Ahrens-Nicklas RC et al., *NEJM* 2026;394(21):2126–2133, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa2601608, PMID 42127296).
- **Why this is the lead item:** the site tracked AAV throughout the cycle, including the *Nature Reviews Drug Discovery* brief published **the day before** in which FDA's own reviewers counted **eight approved AAV in vivo products against more than 300 INDs**. Those eight products wrote the post-approval framework this investigation now tests. On consecutive days the agency's reviewers published their own account of AAV review and the agency opened a malignancy investigation into the oldest and most widely used AAV product on the market — with the vector's integration risk confirmed by molecular analysis in two patients' tumour tissue rather than remaining theoretical.

### 2. Pooled analysis finds no persistent cardiac injury signal with delandistrogene moxeparvovec over one to five years

- **Primary source:** Veerapandiyan A et al., "Cardiac Safety Outcomes in Delandistrogene Moxeparvovec Clinical Trials for Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy with Up to 5 Years of Follow-up," *Cardiology and Therapy* 15(3):301–317 (doi 10.1007/s40119-026-00457-5, open access CC BY-NC) — HTTP 200.
- **Pooled dataset:** **218 patients** across studies 101, 102, ENDEAVOR and EMBARK, of whom **216 received active gene transfer therapy**; follow-up of one to five years, evaluating adverse events, routine echocardiography, serial high-sensitivity troponin I, electrocardiography and cardiac MRI. Cardiac complications were **generally infrequent, transient and responsive to medical management**, with no evidence of persistent treatment-related cardiac injury.
- **Trial mix:** open-label Phase 1/2a study 101 (four participants); randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study 102 (41 patients); multi-cohort open-label Phase 1b ENDEAVOR (48 patients); Phase 3 randomized crossover EMBARK (125 participants). All required stable corticosteroid dosing before screening, except a subset of young children aged under a stated threshold.
- **Why cardiac surveillance was the open question:** the AAVrh74 vector uses an MHCK7-driven micro-dystrophin cassette with an enhancer active in cardiac muscle, specifically to stabilise or slow cardiac progression; because immune responses against capsid or transgene could theoretically provoke myocardial inflammation, and published longitudinal cardiac data were limited, experts had called for systematic tracking of left ventricular performance and cardiac biomarkers after systemic infusion. Preclinical rodent work had shown improved cardiac parameters and lower troponin, while some AAV studies had noted myocardial inflammatory changes.
- **Boundary carried:** a pooled retrospective analysis across open-label and randomised studies of different sizes with no untreated control group of comparable follow-up; it establishes the absence of a persistent injury signal, not a benefit claim.
- **Note on timing:** the paper itself was issued July 1, 2026 (print September 2026) and was written up by Bioengineer on November 7 — surfaced by this cycle's scan, not a new publication this week. The item presents it as the pooled cardiac-safety evidence it is, without dating it to the current cycle.

## Notes from this cycle

- **A product page can be the primary.** FDA's Zolgensma product page lists the safety notice under "Latest Safety Information" — the notice itself sits on a CBER safety URL that is not in FDA's medical-product-safety-information index, and FDA's press-announcement pages 404'd from this environment. **When a regulatory story keeps failing on the obvious routes, read the product page rather than the safety index.**
- **Holding an item across cycles was right.** This story ran a dozen outlets for four cycles with no primary, and the item was published the moment FDA's own notice appeared — with the molecular detail (two cases of demonstrated vector DNA integration in tumour tissue) that no headline carried. Coverage volume bought nothing; the regulator's document supplied everything.
- **A pooled retrospective analysis still answers a real question.** The delandistrophin cardiac dataset spans four trials and up to five years of follow-up; publishing it with its design limits stated is more useful than leaving the question open because no single trial reported it.
- **Scope is enforced by the modality, not the newsworthiness.** A first-in-human result presented at AHA with 31 patients was still held, because an siRNA is a drug and this bulletin covers cell and gene therapies.
