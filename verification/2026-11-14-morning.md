### 1. Shanghai publishes the findings of its investigation into the gene-editing study in which a child died

- **Primary source:** [Global Times, "Shanghai probes gene-editing study after child's death, finds research irregularities and oversight failures," November 13, 2026](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202611/1374249.shtml) — HTTP 200, body read directly. The article reports the findings **released by Shanghai authorities on Friday, November 13**, and cites **CCTV News** as its source for the specific findings. This is a state-media report on an official investigation, not the investigation document itself; that distinction is stated in the item.
- **What happened, in the order the investigation sets out:** the child was diagnosed with **Snijders Blok-Campeau syndrome**, a rare neurodevelopmental disorder with no effective treatment. After several major hospitals failed to help, the family contacted a researcher surnamed **Qiu** at the **Songjiang Research Institute of Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine** in July 2023, hoping to develop a customised gene-editing therapy targeting the central nervous system. Qiu's team did preclinical work; **Xinhua Hospital's ethics committee approved the clinical study on January 2, 2025**; the child and family signed an informed consent form on **February 20**; the child was injected at the hospital on **March 24** and **died on March 31** despite resuscitation. The hospital's ethics committee determined the death was caused by **thrombotic microangiopathy**, a complication of gene therapy, and called for the study to be terminated.
- **Findings against the research team:** the preclinical team led by Qiu **failed to strictly comply with relevant rules and procedures**; it **failed to promptly inform the hospital's clinical study leader of safety findings from non-human primate experiments**, so that warning signals in the safety report did not receive due attention; and it **failed to promptly disclose the completed February 2025 non-human primate safety report, or the child's death, when publishing the paper**.
- **Findings on data:** the investigation found **no evidence of data fabrication**, but identified **academic irregularities including inconsistencies between the actual research methods and those reported in the paper, and the unexplained presentation of data from the same batch at multiple stages**.
- **Findings against the hospital:** deficiencies in scientific and ethical review — the ethics committee approved the study **despite insufficient toxicological and safety evidence and the research team's failure to submit requested supplementary materials** — and although the informed consent form disclosed the possibility of serious complications and adverse events, it **did not specifically emphasize the risk of death**.
- **Sanctions:** the two researchers surnamed **Qiu and Yang were dismissed and barred for five years** from undertaking or participating in science and technology programmes and from receiving awards and honours, and both were entered in the database of serious research integrity violations. Other personnel received disciplinary penalties and the involved hospitals were subjected to administrative penalties.
- **The funding question:** the investigation found that **the child's family provided 5.337 million yuan (about $800,000) in research funding through donations and gave cash and gifts worth approximately 1.06 million yuan to several individuals involved in the research**; those cash and gifts have since been returned or turned over in accordance with regulation.
- **The forward-looking part:** Shanghai authorities have launched **special inspections and rectification efforts covering clinical research into new biomedical technologies**, emphasizing the high risks of clinical studies involving cell and gene therapies and the need for stronger oversight.
- **Registry (v2 API; robots.txt read first this session):** **NCT06860672** is a **terminated** early Phase 1 study of a single intrathecal injection of a **dual-vector AAV-CHD3-R1025W base editor** for developmental disorders caused by the R1025W mutation in CHD3, **1 participant actual**, sponsor **Yongguo Yu**, actual start **February 19, 2025**, **actual** primary completion and completion **March 31, 2025**, last update posted July 30, 2026, **one site — Xinhua Hospital affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine — in China**, conditions listed as developmental delay disorder, intellectual disability and rare diseases. **The registry record and the investigation agree on the dates and the site.** The item notes that a single-patient trial that ran for 40 days and ended in a death is itself the clearest single number in the story.
- **Duplicate check:** the site had **zero** prior mentions of NCT06860672, CHD3, Snijders Blok-Campeau, Songjiang or Xinhua on any of the five pages. Its existing China gene-editing coverage (`update-oct-22-nature-invivo-cart-china`, and the Nov. 1 in vivo DMD death item) concerns **different trials** — the HuidaGene HG302 programme in Duchenne at Shanghai Children's Medical Center, and the VGB-Ex01 trial in Tianjin — so this is new coverage and not a rewrite.

### 2. satri-cel enters China's Commercial Health Insurance Innovative Drug List

- **Primary source:** [CARsgen release, November 14, 2026 (PR Newswire)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/carsgens-car-t-product-satri-cel-included-in-chinas-commercial-health-insurance-innovative-drug-list-302939514.html) — HTTP 200, body read directly.
- **What was announced:** **satri-cel (satricabtagene autoleucel, CT041)**, CARsgen's autologous Claudin18.2 CAR T-cell therapy, has been included in the **Commercial Health Insurance Innovative Drug List (2026)** issued by China's **National Healthcare Security Administration** and the **Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security**, for patients with Claudin18.2-positive, HER2-negative advanced gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma who have failed at least two prior lines of therapy.
- **Why the mechanism of the list matters:** the CHIIDL exists because China's **basic medical insurance scheme does not cover innovative drugs above its basic mandate**, so the list creates a payment basis for commercial insurance and mutual medical aid. Per the release, satri-cel is **the first CAR T-cell therapy product for solid tumours to be included in the CHIIDL since the list was established** — and satri-cel is itself the world's first approved CAR-T for a solid tumour, NMPA-approved in June 2026 for the same indication.
- **What the company says it will do with it:** support the design and implementation of commercial insurance products and accelerate market access at healthcare institutions and standardised clinical use, on the premise of ensuring product quality and stable supply.
- **Pipeline expansion:** satri-cel is being moved into earlier-line and perioperative treatment, with an ongoing Phase 1 pancreatic-cancer adjuvant trial in China (NCT05911217), an IIT for consolidation after adjuvant therapy in resected G/GEJA (NCT06857786) and an IIT for sequential therapy after first-line treatment (NCT07179484).
- **Duplicate check:** the site has carried satri-cel since October 26 (`pub-ind-carsgen-satri-cel-adjuvant`, covering the ESMO 2026 adjuvant data), but a grep for "CHIIDL" and "Commercial Health Insurance Innovative" across all five pages returns **zero** hits before this cycle. **The product is not new; the reimbursement pathway is.**
- **Scope note:** this is a Chinese national reimbursement-list development, not an FDA or EMA action.

### 3. A registry-based read on where rare-disease gene therapy actually stands

- **Primary source:** [Han R, Zhang Y, Liu Y, Tian M, Lu Y, "Clinical landscape of gene therapy for rare diseases: a registry-based analysis of clinical trials and FDA-approved products," *Gene Therapy*, published online November 13, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43026208/) — doi 10.1038/s41434-026-00658-6, PMID 43026208. Abstract read in full via eutils.
- **Method:** FDA product records, Orphanet classifications, and clinical trial records retrieved through **Gene Therapy Net**, whose core data derive from ClinicalTrials.gov, with an **October 2025 cutoff**. The paper analysed publicly available regulatory and registry data, involved no individual-level participant data, and therefore required no ethics approval; the authors declare no competing interests.
- **Findings:** of **1,464 registered studies, 646 (44.13%) addressed 175 distinct rare diseases**, and **annual initiation of rare-disease studies rose from 21 in 2015 to 73 in 2024**. Hemophilia A and B, retinitis pigmentosa, Duchenne muscular dystrophy and sickle cell disease were the most frequently studied indications. Within the rare-disease cohort, **547 studies (84.67%) were interventional**, **408 (63.16%) were early phase I, phase I or phase I/II**, and **402 (62.23%) permitted paediatric enrolment**. **The United States was listed as a study location in 410 studies (63.47%)**. Of **30 approved gene therapy products identified, 24 (80.00%) had at least one rare-disease indication, collectively covering 16 indications**.
- **The authors' conclusion is a caution, not a celebration, and the item quotes it:** the predominance of early-phase studies and geographic concentration **highlight priorities for later-phase evaluation and broader international participation**.
- **Why it belongs here rather than as a market note:** the 63% early-phase figure is the quantitative form of the gap this bulletin tracks — a registry full of first-in-human studies and a short list of approved products — and the 63% US-site concentration is the quantitative form of the geographic concentration the in vivo lentiviral CAR-T field runs into.
- **Duplicate check:** the site had **zero** prior mentions of this analysis, of "Clinical landscape of gene therapy", or of Gene Therapy Net.
