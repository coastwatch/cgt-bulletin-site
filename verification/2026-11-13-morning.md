## Published

### 1. Intellia reports the Phase 3 data behind the first in vivo CRISPR filing

- **Primary source:** [Intellia release, November 12, 2026 (GlobeNewswire via The Manila Times)](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/11/12/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/intellia-therapeutics-presents-additional-positive-clinical-and-patient-reported-data-for-lonvoguran-ziclumeran-lonvo-z-at-acaai-2026/2468780) — HTTP 200, body read directly. Corroborated from the [SEC 8-K Exhibit 99.1 investor deck](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652130/000119312526458422/ntla-ex99_1.htm) (HTTP 200 with a declared User-Agent, read directly); the [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652130/000119312526458422/ntla-20261112.htm) is Items 2.02 and 7.01 furnishing the deck for the "Readying Lonvo-z for Patients with HAE" webcast.
- **Framing:** Intellia presented at **seven presentations at the ACAAI 2026 Annual Scientific Meeting** in Phoenix, Arizona.
- **Phase 3 HAELO efficacy, denominators as the company states them:**
  - **Key secondary endpoint, attack-free weeks 5 to 28: 87% reduction versus placebo (95% CI 78% to 93%; p<0.0001).**
  - **Primary endpoint, mean attack-rate reduction: 62% versus 11% on placebo, odds ratio 12.8 (95% CI 3.5 to 47.6; p<0.0001).**
  - All other key secondary endpoints met with high statistical significance (p<0.0001).
  - **Randomised arms: lonvo-z n=52 against placebo n=28.**
- **Subgroup consistency:** all prespecified subgroups showed consistent benefit in attack-rate reduction favouring lonvo-z, regardless of disease severity or prior long-term prophylaxis.
- **The long-term-prophylaxis subgroup, which is the commercially material number:** among the **35 HAELO patients taking long-term prophylaxis at study entry who were randomised to lonvo-z**, the mean HAE attack rate fell **81%** during the efficacy observation period versus their rate on prior prophylaxis, and **63% achieved complete control**, more than tripling the attack-free rate they had on prior prophylaxis.
- **Safety, in the company's words:** **all treatment-emergent adverse events were mild or moderate and there were no serious adverse events observed in the lonvo-z arm.**
- **Patient-reported outcomes:** HAELO met its key secondary endpoint of change from baseline to week 28 in Angioedema Quality of Life (AE-QoL) total score — **23.5-point mean reduction for lonvo-z against 6.5 points for placebo**, with the company noting a 6-point reduction is the threshold for a clinically important difference — with clinically meaningful improvements across all four AE-QoL domains, sustained over time. In interviews with 23 Phase 1/2 patients at a median 2.4 years of follow-up, **all reported their HAE was well controlled after lonvo-z, against 48% before treatment**; **86% changed their definition of well controlled**; and **22 of 23 (96%) described lonvo-z as an optimal therapy**.
- **Durability:** in Phase 1/2, mean plasma kallikrein reduction was **84% at four weeks, maintained through up to four years**; the proportion of patients attack-free after one year was **at least 90% in each month** with up to four years of follow-up; **100% of patients receiving 50 mg across Phase 1/2 and HAELO were long-term-prophylaxis-free at the data cutoff**; and the estimated probability of beginning an attack-free and prophylaxis-free interval lasting six months rose from **81% at six months to 93% at 24 months**. Cell number did not correlate with CSF BDNF after the third administration (ρ = 0.14, 39 patients with paired samples).
- **A companion finding from the same meeting, reported as a separate observation:** a US claims analysis of **538 HAE patients on long-term prophylaxis over a median 1.2 years found almost 40% were non-adherent**, with similar attack rates in adherent and non-adherent groups, indicating breakthrough attacks despite prophylaxis.
- **Registry (v2 API; robots.txt read first this session):** NCT06634420 (HAELO) — **completed** Phase 3, **80 participants actual**, sponsor Intellia Therapeutics, actual start January 15, 2025, **actual** primary completion February 10, 2026, estimated completion September 2027, last update posted October 8, 2026, 29 sites.
- **What this item does not change, stated plainly:** the BLA acceptance, priority review and **March 10, 2027 PDUFA date** were published by this bulletin on November 5 (`pub-ind-intellia-lonvoz-hae-pdufa`) and are unchanged. This item adds the efficacy, safety and patient-reported data behind them. FDA has advised it is **not currently planning to hold an advisory committee** to discuss the application.
- **Duplicate check:** the site carried the regulatory status but **no** Phase 3 efficacy figure. A page-wide grep confirmed the absence of "attack-free", "Week 28", "AE-QoL" and the 87% reduction; the single existing "87%" is an unrelated Xencor disease-control rate and the single "62%" an unrelated ophthalmologist survey figure.

### 2. Intrathecal lineage-negative cells in ALS: the authors say the apparent plateau was completion bias

- **Primary source:** [Gołąb-Janowska M, Wełnicka-Wesołowska A, Baumert B, et al., "Functional Status, Quality of Life and Survival After Repeated Intrathecal Administration of Autologous Lineage-Negative Cells in Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis," *Journal of Clinical Medicine*, published October 14, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42941452/) — 15(20):7937, doi 10.3390/jcm15207937, PMID 42941452, PMCID PMC13705995. Abstract read in full via eutils; the **open-access full text was read directly via the PMC XML endpoint** (`eutils efetch db=pmc id=PMC13705995`), which is where the registration number, the ethics approval code and the recruitment window come from. PubMed returns 203 to plain curl and PMC and MDPI return 403 to every User-Agent tried from this environment — bot guards, not dead links.
- **Design:** prospective, **open-label, non-randomised, single-centre** study of **intrathecal autologous bone marrow-derived lineage-negative (LIN-) cells**, approved by the Pomeranian Medical University bioethics committee (**KB-0012/06/10, 25 January 2010**). Patients were recruited and treated between **February 2018 and February 2019** and followed for two years. Of **45** qualified patients, **42 received all three administrations** at six-week intervals and **23 completed the 24-week assessment schedule**, with 19 stopping attendance because of deterioration.
- **Results, as the authors report them:** among the 23 completers, **Norris scale scores were stable until week 13 and then declined (p = 0.00004)**, and the **ALSAQ-40** quality-of-life measure followed the same pattern (p = 0.0019), while **ALSFRS-R — the standard efficacy measure in ALS trials — declined at a constant 0.5 points per month**. Across all 42 treated patients the Norris score fell by **0.95 points per month (95% CI 0.52 to 1.38)**, so, in the authors' own explanation, **the apparent plateau was confined to the completers, who had milder baseline disease**.
- **The caveats the authors state themselves are the substance of the item:** dose-related differences in quality of life were **confounded by baseline imbalance**, and the survival analyses were **exploratory**. **No adverse event was recorded across 126 procedures, but harms were not systematically graded.** The authors conclude that these exploratory findings **do not demonstrate efficacy** and that a **sham-controlled trial with blinded raters** is required.
- **Registry (v2 API):** the paper names the registration, **NCT02193893** — "Safety/Efficacy Study for the Biological Treatment of Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis With Autologous Stem/Progenitor Cells", sponsor **Pomeranian Medical University Szczecin**, **Phase 1, 50 participants estimated, overall status UNKNOWN**, start January 2010, estimated primary completion July 2017 and estimated completion December 2017, **last update posted July 18, 2014**, one site in Poland, with cell-based therapeutics and symptomatic neurological treatment as the two arms. **Two things the item flags:** the trial was registered in 2010 and ran to 2019, so the published data come from a study whose registry entry is more than a decade stale, and the "UNKNOWN" status reflects that the sponsor has not updated the record, not that the conduct is unknown.
- **Duplicate check:** the site had no prior coverage of Pomeranian, lineage-negative cells, or this ALS programme. A sweep of ALS-condition interventional studies with a cell intervention returned 50 records; the only registered cell-therapy ALS Phase 3 (Corestemchemon's ALSummit, NCT04745299, n=123, completed) has no new publication, and its "subgroup benefit" was reported in September 2025 — see held, below.

## Notes from this cycle

- **A regulator's page can be re-fetched and re-read to prove that nothing changed.** The Zolgensma hold has run four cycles on the reasoning "FDA's notice may have been updated." Fetching it directly this cycle — full body, `dateModified`, and the communications index — establishes that it has **not** been updated since November 6 and that the "benefits outweigh risks" language is the same text already published on Nov. 7. That converts a repeated hold into a settled one and stops the next cycle re-reading it.
- **A trade headline that says a regulator "says" something is not the regulator's statement.** The Nov. 13 SMA News Today piece reads as new ("FDA says") but every sentence in it is from the November 6 notice. The item it appears to announce was published by this bulletin six days earlier.
- **The most valuable half of a company's data release is often the subgroup that survives its own denominator problem.** Intellia's headline endpoint is the 87% attack-free-week reduction; the commercially decisive number is the **81% fall in attack rate among the 35 patients who came in on long-term prophylaxis** — a population whose attacks the existing prophylactic drugs failed to stop, and where the company also reported ~40% non-adherence in a 538-patient claims analysis. Published side by side.
- **Let the authors explain their own result.** The ALS paper's most important sentence is the authors' own: across all 42 treated patients the Norris score *fell*, so the plateau was confined to completers with milder baseline disease, and the findings "do not demonstrate efficacy." Quoting that is worth more than any paraphrase of the headline.
- **A registry record can be a decade stale and still be the only anchor.** NCT02193893 was last updated in 2014 for a trial that ran to 2019, and carries "UNKNOWN" status because nobody updated it. The item reports the staleness rather than treating the record as current.
- **Two items is a fine morning.** The window contained two genuine, primary-verified clinical developments. The rest was already covered, out of scope, pre-cutoff, or without a primary.
