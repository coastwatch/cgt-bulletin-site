### 1. Pfizer licenses three in vivo CAR-T autoimmune programmes from Starna for up to $2.6 billion

- **Primary source:** [Reuters report carried by ETPharma, "Pfizer licenses CAR-T therapies from China's Starna in up to $2.6 billion deal," published November 19, 2026](http://pharma.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/pharma-industry/pfizer-licenses-car-t-therapies-from-chinas-starna-in-up-to-2-6-billion-deal/135975505) — HTTP 200, body read directly. The deal announcement itself is carried on [Starna's own corporate news page](https://www.starnatx.com/), dated **November 18, 2026** ("中国苏州/上海和美国纽约，[2026/11/18]"), which was read directly. Pfizer's own newsroom carries no release for it and Business Wire is unreachable from this environment.
- **The terms:** Pfizer pays Starna **$330 million upfront** and could pay **up to $2.3 billion more** on development, regulatory and sales milestones; Starna receives royalties on any approved product.
- **Scope and geography:** rights to develop and commercialise **three early-stage autoimmune disease programmes outside Greater China**, with **initial clinical trial supplies to be manufactured at Pfizer's US facilities**. The manufacturing clause is the part a supply-chain reader should not skip.
- **The platform, from Starna's own description:** **extrahepatic tissue-targeted lipid nanoparticle delivery** — Starna organises large lipid libraries into thousands of nanoparticle formulations, screened with AI computation and validated with wet-lab methods into tissue-specific LNPs; coupling ligands such as antibodies or proteins to those LNPs gives **cell-selective LNPs**, which the company describes as a route to in vivo cell therapy. Pfizer's release describes the licensed therapies as in vivo CAR-T that genetically reprograms immune cells inside the body to target and eliminate disease-causing immune cells.
- **The stage of the assets — and a discrepancy worth reporting.** Reuters states the therapies **"have not yet entered human trials."** The registry is more precise than that, and the item says both. A sponsor sweep for Starna returns **seven records**. The autoimmune ones are **NCT07363954** (STR-P004 for immune-mediated kidney disease, not yet recruiting, 9 estimated, start February 2, 2026, no locations listed), **NCT07655453** (STR-P005 in relapsed/refractory autoimmune disease, not yet recruiting, 36 estimated, start August 1, 2026), **NCT07605637** (early clinical study of STR-P004 in relapsed/refractory autoimmune disease, not yet recruiting, 18 estimated, start June 1, 2026, sponsored by Beijing GoBroad Hospital) and **NCT07143617** (exploratory clinical study of STR-P004 in autoimmune disease, **recruiting**, 39 estimated, actual start September 16, 2025, sponsored by Beijing GoBroad Hospital). The oncology records are NCT07003178 (STR-P004 in non-Hodgkin lymphoma, early Phase 1, not yet recruiting, 30 estimated, one site in China) and NCT07245251 (STR-P004 in B-ALL, not yet recruiting, 11 estimated); the seventh is an unrelated RSV vaccine study.
- **The distinction the item draws:** *"have not yet entered human trials"* is true of the **licensed autoimmune programmes**, all of which are not yet recruiting. It is **not** true of Starna's STR-P004 platform as a whole, which has a recruiting autoimmune record and an oncology record, both still undosed.
- **Duplicate check:** the site had **zero** prior mentions of Starna, STR-P004 or STR-P005 on any of the five pages, and no ledger mentions them.

### 2. Lexeo reports interim LX2020 data in PKP2 arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy

- **Primary source:** [SEC EDGAR — Lexeo 8-K Exhibit 99.1, November 16, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1907108/000119312526462015/lxeo-ex99_1.htm) — HTTP 200 with a declared User-Agent, body read directly. The release is datelined **NEW YORK – November 16, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE)**.
- **Efficacy:** across **ten participants**, including **nine with at least 12 months of follow-up**, LX2020 was generally well tolerated and associated with clinically meaningful reductions in arrhythmia burden. In the **high-dose cohorts**, participants achieved an **80% mean reduction in non-sustained ventricular tachycardia** and a **44% mean reduction in premature ventricular contractions** at latest assessment, alongside improvement in right-ventricular function measures and patient-reported symptoms.
- **Safety:** generally well tolerated across the ten participants **with no clinically significant complement activation** — the point that carries most weight for an AAVrh.10hPKP2 cardiac product, given complement activation is the mechanistic question for systemic AAV dosing to the heart.
- **The regulatory development and its limits:** the company states **FDA is open to guideline-defined NSVT as a primary surrogate endpoint** to support potential accelerated approval, and that Lexeo is now designing a pivotal study on that basis. **The release states plainly what is not settled:** the final study design, including the statistical analysis plan and the confirmatory strategy, **remains subject to future discussions with FDA**. The item calls this FDA openness to an endpoint, not an agreed accelerated-approval pathway.
- **Registry (v2 API; robots.txt read first this session):** **NCT06109181** is a Phase 1/2 study of LX2020 in arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy due to a PKP2 pathogenic variant, **active, not recruiting, 10 participants estimated**, sponsor Lexeo Therapeutics, actual start **February 29, 2024**, estimated primary completion **December 2026**, estimated completion March 2027, **last update posted November 12, 2025**, **five US sites**, with LX2020 described as an adeno-associated viral vector encoding the human plakophilin-2 gene (AAVrh.10hPKP2). **Two things the item flags:** the registry still reads 10 estimated while the release reports ten treated with nine followed beyond a year, and the record has not been updated in a year.
- **Financials:** cash, cash equivalents and investments of **$219.1 million** at September 30, 2026, expected to provide operational runway into 2028.
- **Duplicate check:** the site carries Lexeo's *Friedreich's ataxia* programme (LX2006, NCT05445323 and NCT07721025) from an earlier cycle, but **zero** prior mentions of LX2020, PKP2 or HEROIC. This is a different product in a different indication.

### 3. Emmecell doses the first patient in EMERALD

- **Primary source:** [Emmecell release via Business Wire, carried by BioSpace, November 18, 2026](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/emmecell-announces-first-patient-dosed-in-emerald-study-of-magnetic-cell-therapy-for-corneal-endothelial-disease) — HTTP 200, body read directly.
- **The study design is the substance:** EMERALD is a **randomised, double-masked, placebo-controlled** US study of two doses of EO2002, with a **primary endpoint measuring the proportion of study eyes gaining at least 15 letters of vision at 26 weeks**. A placebo-controlled design in a cell therapy is unusual, and the item says so.
- **The product and its delivery:** EO2002 is an **allogeneic human corneal endothelial cell therapy** given by **single intracameral injection**, using Emmecell's **Magnetic Cell Delivery** platform — **magnetically labelled cells plus an external magnetic patch** to guide cells to the corneal endothelium and enhance retention. The company positions it as a minimally invasive alternative to corneal transplantation, including endothelial keratoplasty, which requires donated corneal tissue and specialised surgical care.
- **Registry (v2 API):** **NCT07729137** is the EMERALD study — **recruiting** Phase 2 of EO2002 in corneal edema secondary to corneal endothelial dysfunction, **121 participants estimated**, **ten US sites**, sponsor Emmecell. Earlier records are NCT04894110 (Phase 1, completed, 42 participants actual, eight US sites) and NCT05587205 (Phase 1, recruiting, 32 estimated, one site in Mexico).
- **What the release does not say, and the item adds:** EMERALD is a 121-participant, ten-site study with no stated enrolment pace or timing, so the first-dosing milestone is a **start, not a near-term readout**.
- **Scope note:** no corneal endothelial product appears on FDA's approved cellular and gene therapy products list, fetched and checked this session.

### 4. Opus Genetics doses the first participant in OPGx-RDH12

- **Primary source:** [Opus Genetics investor press release, November 19, 2026](https://ir.opusgtx.com/press-releases/detail/553/opus-genetics-announces-first-participant-dosed-in-phase-12-clinical-trial-of-opgx-rdh12-gene-therapy-for-rare-inherited-childhood-blindness) — HTTP 200, body read directly. Dateline **RESEARCH TRIANGLE PARK, N.C., Nov. 19, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE)**.
- **The milestone:** the **first participant has been dosed** in the Phase 1/2 trial of OPGx-RDH12, an investigational gene therapy for **RDH12-associated Leber congenital amaurosis** — progressive vision loss and blindness beginning in early childhood, affecting approximately **2,500 individuals in the US**.
- **What it completes:** the dosing follows **FDA clearance of the IND application in October 2026**, and advances Opus's **third gene therapy programme** into clinical development. **This site published that IND clearance on October 21** as a trial-start milestone; the item states the relationship explicitly rather than presenting the clearance as new.
- **Product and endpoints:** OPGx-RDH12 is designed to **deliver a functional copy of the RDH12 gene directly to photoreceptor cells in the retina**, with the goal of preserving retinal function and vision. The Phase 1/2 will evaluate safety and tolerability along with **exploratory measures of retinal structure and visual function** — the word "exploratory" is the company's own and the item keeps it.
- **Registry (v2 API):** **NCT07681778** is a **recruiting** Phase 1/2 of subretinally injected OPGx-RDH12-1001 for LCA-associated inherited retinal degeneration, **10 participants estimated**, sponsor Opus Genetics, Inc., start **October 2026**, estimated primary completion **December 2032**, last update posted **October 30, 2026**, **four US sites**. **The item flags the planning numbers:** a ten-participant, four-site Phase 1/2 with an estimated primary completion at the end of 2032, and a registry last updated three weeks before the announcement.
- **Programme support:** the clinical programme is supported by a strategic collaboration with the **Global RDH12 Alliance**, bringing together the RDH12 Fund for Sight and Eyes on the Future.
- **Scope note:** no Leber congenital amaurosis product appears on FDA's approved cellular and gene therapy products list, fetched and checked this session.

## Notes from this cycle

- **A five-day publication gap produced no stale content, and that is worth stating explicitly.** The refresh cron only updates timestamps, so a dormant host leaves the site's content unchanged rather than wrong. The gap was caused by session lane timeouts and then host sleep, and the fix was to re-establish the baseline commit and re-run — the same discipline as any other cycle.

- **"Not yet in human trials" is a claim that the registry can make more precise than the wire copy can.** Reuters' formulation for the Starna deal is true of the licensed autoimmune programmes but not of the platform, which has a recruiting autoimmune record and an oncology record, both undosed. Publishing both, and the sweep behind them, is more useful than either alone.

The item keeps that distinction rather than reporting a pivotal strategy as agreed.
- **A first-dosing milestone says nothing about when a readout arrives.** Emmecell's EMERALD is a 121-participant, ten-site study and Opus's OPGx-RDH12 trial has an estimated primary completion in December 2032. Both numbers are in the items.
