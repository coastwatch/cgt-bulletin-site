### 1. Multicenter characterization of enterocolitis associated with BCMA-directed CAR T-cell therapy

- **Primary source:** [Mikkilineni et al., Nature Medicine, “Multicenter characterization of enterocolitis associated with BCMA-directed CAR T cell therapy”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04783-y), DOI 10.1038/s41591-026-04783-y — HTTP 200; publisher article/abstract and reported results read directly. Published November 19, 2026. [PubMed PMID 43058222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43058222/) returned HTTP 203 with a cookies-required interstitial in this environment; it was removed from the bulletin links, which retain the directly read Nature primary.
- The US Multiple Myeloma Immunotherapy Consortium retrospectively adjudicated 54 suspected IEC-EC cases from eight institutions; 41 were classified as IEC-EC (31 confirmed by anti-camelid variable heavy-chain immunohistochemistry, 10 probable). The paper separately reports a treated-population cumulative incidence of 3.0% (39/1,308 cilta-cel recipients; 95% CI 2.1–4.1%) and 12-month competing-risk incidence of 3.0%. The case-adjudication denominator and incidence denominator are distinct and are kept distinct in the item.
- Among affected patients, median symptom onset was 75 days after infusion; Grade ≥3 diarrhoea occurred in 34/40 with diarrhoea (85%), and 30/41 (73%) required parenteral nutrition. At last follow-up, 13/41 (32%) had resolved, 11/41 (27%) had ongoing symptoms, and 17/41 (41%) had died with active IEC-EC. Normal upper endoscopy occurred in 9/22 scoped patients (41%) despite diagnostic duodenal biopsy findings. These figures and denominators match the Nature article.
- **Label cross-check:** [DailyMed CARVYKTI prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=7d040b91-3fb8-41db-ba7f-60a36f06e2c2) — HTTP 200; SPL read directly. The label revised October 2025 already lists IEC-EC under Warnings and Precautions §5.9, including severe/prolonged diarrhoea, abdominal pain and weight loss; parenteral nutrition; possible fatal perforation or sepsis; specialist referral; and further evaluation of refractory cases. The bulletin therefore says the paper quantifies a toxicity the label already names; it does not imply the paper caused a label change.
- This is a retrospective clinical characterization of an adverse event in an approved CAR T-cell therapy, not a new regulatory action. It is distinct from the companion mechanistic Nature Medicine paper already published in the Nov. 19 bulletin; the companion concerned persistence/clonality, while this report characterizes clinical burden and diagnostic findings.
- **Timing:** Nature dates the article November 19, before the previous morning bulletin update. It is transparently labeled as late-found, not as new after the Nov. 20 morning cutoff.

### 2. Verismo licensed Stanford PACK-IT technology for an in-vivo KIR-CAR development effort

- **Primary source:** [Verismo Therapeutics / PR Newswire release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/verismo-therapeutics-licenses-in-vivo-car-t-engineering-technology-to-extend-its-novel-multi-chain-kir-car-platform-302943157.html) — HTTP 200; release body read directly. PR Newswire metadata dates it November 18, 2026, 13:00 UTC (08:00 EST).
- The company says it licensed Stanford Medicine's PACK-IT (Programmable Antigen-Mediated Cellular Knock-in of T cell) technology, using engineered T-cell-targeted lentiviral particles, to extend its multi-chain KIR-CAR platform toward in-vivo oncology candidates. Preclinical evidence referenced in the release was presented at AACR 2025. The item attributes intended access/cost benefits to the company and does not present the platform as clinically validated.
- **Registry (ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API; robots.txt read before the detailed record checks):** [NCT05568680](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05568680) — HTTP 200, recruiting Phase 1 SynKIR-110 in mesothelin-expressing ovarian cancer, cholangiocarcinoma or mesothelioma; 42 estimated participants; Verismo Therapeutics sponsor; five U.S. locations; last update posted June 24, 2026. [NCT06544265](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06544265) — HTTP 200, recruiting Phase 1 SynKIR-310 in relapsed/refractory B-cell NHL; 36 estimated participants; Verismo sponsor; five U.S. locations; last update posted April 14, 2026. Neither record lists an in-vivo arm. The item explicitly distinguishes the proposed platform extension from the registered ex-vivo clinical programmes.
