### 1. FDA's own reviewers published their account of how the agency has handled AAV gene therapies

- **Primary source:** Bing S, Argaw T, Kaushal M, Mazor R, "Regulatory lessons from FDA review of approved AAV gene therapies," *Nature Reviews Drug Discovery* Biobusiness Brief, published November 6, 2026 (https://www.nature.com/articles/d41573-026-00178-3, DOI 10.1038/d41573-026-00178-3) — HTTP 200, body read directly. **All four authors are staff in FDA's Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research**, which is what makes it authoritative about FDA's own practice; the item states plainly that it is an agency perspective rather than independent analysis.
- **Counts as published:** between 2017 and 2025 FDA approved **eight AAV-based in vivo gene therapy products** under BLAs, and by the end of 2025 the agency had received **over 300 new IND submissions** using AAV gene delivery. The article analyses public and FDA data on regulatory timelines, expedited pathways, communication patterns and postmarketing obligations.
- **Why it belongs here:** eight approved products against 300+ INDs is the clearest statement yet of the shape of the field — the regulatory science around AAV was written by eight products, and the post-approval obligations attached to them are what every one of those 300 submissions will eventually be measured against. For a manufacturing-facing bulletin this is the specification, not background.
- **Live context carried in the item:** the brief appeared one day after FDA's finalized potency-assurance guidance for CGT products (reported by this bulletin on November 3) and amid active reporting that the agency is examining tumour risk in children treated with Novartis' Zolgensma, an AAV9 product — the same thread: FDA is currently writing the post-approval framework for a modality whose approved products are now generating safety signals the 2017–2025 cohort did not contain.
- **Boundary carried:** a Biobusiness Brief, not a full review — the article's own scope statement is that it analyses public and FDA data and highlights lessons learned.

### 2. Precision BioSciences: PBGENE-DMD clears its first eight weeks with a positive DSMB review

- **Primary source:** [Precision BioSciences press release, November 5, 2026, filed as Exhibit 99.3 to Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1357874/000162828026070542/ex993dmddsmbsafetyreview11.htm) — HTTP 200, body read directly.
- The independent Data Safety Monitoring Board completed its review of safety data from the **first patient treated with PBGENE-DMD** in the Phase 1/2 FUNCTION-DMD study: **no serious adverse events and no adverse events of special interest through week eight**, and the DSMB endorsed proceeding with dosing additional patients in Part 1. The company describes the eight-week window as key for monitoring the early safety events known to be associated with AAV-based therapies.
- **Mechanism, which is what distinguishes it from the approved field:** PBGENE-DMD uses **two complementary ARCUS nucleases delivered in a single AAV to excise exons 45–55** of the dystrophin gene, aiming to restore a near full-length functional dystrophin protein native to the body rather than a foreign truncated microdystrophin. Mutations between exons 45 and 55 represent up to 60% of boys with DMD.
- **Registry (v2 API; robots.txt read first this session):** NCT07429240 (study ID PBGENE-DMD-01) — recruiting Phase 1/2a multi-center, open-label study of a single dose of PBGENE-DMD, 18 estimated participants, sponsor Precision BioSciences, actual start 2026-04-24, estimated primary completion 2029-11, last update posted 2026-10-21, two US sites; primary outcome is incidence, severity and causality of treatment-emergent and serious adverse events.
- **What is not verified:** this is a safety milestone in **one patient** — no efficacy data, no dystrophin expression result and no functional outcome is in the public record, and the registry still lists two sites against an 18-participant target. The company states it expects safety and efficacy updates from FUNCTION-DMD throughout 2027.

### 3. Precision BioSciences: PBGENE-HBV Phase 1 data, with cccDNA elimination and a move to Phase 2

- **Primary source:** [Precision BioSciences press release, November 5, 2026, filed as Exhibit 99.1 to Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1357874/000162828026070542/ex99120261105precisionaasl.htm) — HTTP 200, body read directly.
- ELIMINATE-B Phase 1 data to be presented in a late-breaking oral at AASLD (November 8, 2026): **complete and durable loss of pgRNA to undetectable in 100% of evaluable patients at the go-forward 0.4 mg/kg dose**, with durable ongoing pgRNA loss in all patients who had at least six months of follow-up. Across all dose levels **11 evaluable patients** achieved pgRNA loss to undetectable and remained so through the cutoff; a first HBeAg-positive patient dosed on study went from a baseline of 3.7-log copies/mL to undetectable; liver biopsies showed a reproducible **1-log and 1.5-log reduction in cccDNA** at 0.4 mg/kg across multiple patients; treatment effect was observed in 100% of evaluable patients by HBsAg reduction. Safety described as well characterised and manageable across **59 administrations in 25 patients, with no dose-limiting toxicities**. Data cutoff September 28, 2026; a Phase 1b data set is expected in the first half of 2027.
- **Registry:** NCT06680232 — recruiting Phase 1, open-label, first-in-human dose-escalation (Part 1) and expansion (Part 2) study of PBGENE-HBV, 45 estimated, sponsor Precision BioSciences, actual start 2024-11-14, estimated primary completion December 2026, last update posted 2026-08-24, seven sites across France, Hong Kong, Moldova, New Zealand, Romania and the United States. Long-term observational follow-up NCT07254208 — enrolling by invitation, 45 estimated, primary completion January 2040.
- **Boundary carried:** undetectable pgRNA is a viral marker, not a durability or safety demonstration — the follow-up study runs to 2040 because the durability question is open — and the company states it will be presenting, not that it has presented, these results.
