### 1. Kyverna submits a rolling BLA for miv-cel in stiff person syndrome

- **Primary source:** [SEC EDGAR — Kyverna 8-K, Exhibit 99.1, November 12, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1994702/000119312526457040/kytx-ex99_1.htm) — HTTP 200 with a declared User-Agent, body read directly. - Kyverna has **completed the submission of a rolling BLA to FDA for miv-cel (mivocabtagene autoleucel, KYV-101) in stiff person syndrome**, seeking **priority review** under the therapy's RMAT designation. The submission rests on the KYSA-8 registrational trial.
- **Company claims, kept as claims:** the release says that if approved, miv-cel would be the first approved therapy for SPS and the first approved CAR T-cell therapy in autoimmune disease. - **Also new:** additional one-year biomarker data from KYSA-8 will be presented at **ASH 2026** — a scheduled presentation, so no biomarker figure is stated.
- **Rest of the franchise:** KYSA-6 (gMG registrational) enrollment on track to complete mid-2027; Phase 2 longer-term follow-up presented September 2026 and at AANEM showed durable benefit up to 1.5 years with no high-grade CRS and no ICANS. The company states it has **elected to deprioritize the KYV-102 program**. RMAT in non-active secondary progressive MS; development strategy in PMS expected by early 2027.
- **Financials:** $170.6M cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities at September 30, 2026, plus drawable Oxford Finance amended loan facility, expected to fund operations into 2028; R&D $27.0M and G&A $15.9M for the quarter; net loss $41.9M, $0.68 per share.

NCT06193889 (KYSA-6) — **recruiting** Phase 2/3 in gMG, 66 participants estimated, actual start August 28, 2024, estimated primary completion September 2027, last update posted July 29, 2026, 22 sites.

## Notes from this cycle

- **SEC EDGAR is the route when a company's IR site is blocked.** Four of this cycle's five items were sourced from EDGAR filings, each read in full: Kyverna's 8-K Exhibit 99.1, enGene's 8-K Exhibit 99.3, Taysha's 8-K Exhibit 99.1, and Capricor's 10-Q. **EDGAR requires a declared User-Agent** — `curl -A "CGT-Bulletin-Research contact@example.com"` returns 200 where a generic browser UA returns 403, so a 403 from sec.gov is a bot filter, not a dead source. The company's own IR sites were 403, 502 or unreachable for several of these issuers, which is exactly why the filing route mattered.
- **The totals string runs one above the element count on every page.** The site's published totals are `153 / 139 / 137 / 137` while the pages carry 152 / 138 / 136 / 136 `class="news-item"` elements. An initial five-item draft assumed 155 / 141 / 139 / 139 and was **wrong** — the correct increment is on the totals string, not the element count. This is the same off-by-one that has bitten every cycle and it should be read off the live page rather than computed.
- **A superlative can be contradicted by a product this bulletin already reported.** enGene's "first and only non-viral plasmid gene therapy" fails against VOCERYQ, which FDA approved on November 1 and **this site published on October 31**. The counter-example was already in the corpus; it only needed a registry-and-approval-list check rather than a news search.
- **A filed 10-Q contains a different and more consequential story than the press release about the same quarter.** For Capricor, the release carried the PDUFA extension and the manufacturing plan; the 10-Q added the workforce reduction, the original August 22 action date, the 2.8-point ejection-fraction difference and the company's own "descriptive in nature" caveat on the open-label extension.
- **The company's own caveat can be stronger than the bulletin's.** The 10-Q's words — "not powered for statistical comparison, had no alpha allocated and are descriptive in nature" — are a sharper characterisation of the 76% OLE slowing than anything the press release offers, and quoting the filing is better than paraphrasing it.
- **A conference announcement with no abstract is publishable only if the item says so.** The AAVantgarde item states that no clinical data are disclosed, that the presentation is scheduled, and that the registry's primary completion date has already passed.
- **A candidate held three cycles running should be recorded as held with its reason, not left to be re-derived.** VectorY now has a decisive reason — the company's own newsroom shows no November 2026 release — which is stronger than the access-block reason from Nov. 11.
- **Scope discipline is a filter, not a formality.** City Therapeutics' first dosing is a real, primary-verified event, and it is still out of scope because CITY-RBP4 is an RNAi oligonucleotide. The reasoning is recorded so the next cycle does not spend the effort again.
- Every published link was checked before commit (all 200 with the appropriate User-Agent); pages passed tag-balance, duplicate-id, placeholder-link and bare-ampersand checks against a pre-edit baseline; the per-note totals ownership assertion passed on all five pages, and each historical note retains its own counts.
